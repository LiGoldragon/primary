# Metaflow dimensions 2: nesting, splitting, mind, speech, stars

This is a research report for flow bad807, delegated by the main flow. It is read-only web research done on 2026-10-04 and continues `metaflow-dimensions.md`. Bracketed tags such as [TU15] point to `## Sources`. Transliteration is IAST. Every Sanskrit term has its English beside it. **S** means the tradition itself states the mapping, and the tag names where. *R* means it is only a reading (an inference, not stated in the source). **Contested** marks a claim on which sources disagree.

## 1. Nesting: the three inside the larger sets

### 1a. Guṇa (strands: sattva clarity, rajas activity, tamas inertia)

**In varṇa (social order): S, but the detail is commentarial.** Gītā 4.13 says the four were made "by division of guṇa and karma (action)". The per-varṇa split below comes from the commentators on Gītā 18.41 [BG1841].
**Contested:** whether this means birth or temperament.

**In avasthā (states): R.** This is derived by chaining Maitrī 5.2 (rajas is Brahmā, sattva is Viṣṇu, tamas is Rudra) [MU52] with the Parabrahma Upaniṣad (Brahmā is waking, Viṣṇu is dream, Maheśvara is deep sleep) [PBU]. The Vedānta "three bodies" doctrine separately ties suṣupti (deep sleep) to the causal body, where tamas dominates [3B].

**In puruṣārtha (aims): R only.** No source found maps them. A possible reading: dharma (right order) to sattva, artha (means) to rajas, kāma (desire) to rajas and tamas, and mokṣa (release) beyond the guṇas.

**In antaḥkaraṇa (inner organ): S. The three do not distribute here.** Pañcadaśī 1.20 makes the whole inner organ from the *total sattva* of the subtle elements [PD1]. In Sāṃkhyakārikā 25, vaikārika (sattvic) ahaṃkāra yields the eleven organs, bhūtādi (tamasic) ahaṃkāra yields the tanmātra (subtle elements), and taijasa (rajasic) ahaṃkāra powers both [SK25].

### 1b. Trimūrti (three forms: Brahmā creator, Viṣṇu preserver, Śiva/Rudra dissolver)

**In avasthā plus turīya: S, but the source is late** (Parabrahma Upaniṣad, 14th or 15th century) [PBU].

| | jāgrat (waking) | svapna (dream) | suṣupti (deep sleep) | turīya (the fourth) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brahmā | ● | | | |
| Viṣṇu | | ● | | |
| Śiva | | | ● (as Maheśvara) | Brahman (the absolute) holds the fourth |

**In oṃ (the sacred syllable).** Maitrī 6.5 makes Brahmā, Rudra and Viṣṇu the "deity-body" of the three mātrā (letters) a, u, m, without a one-to-one order [MU65]. Māṇḍūkya 9 to 12 maps a, u and m to waking, dream and sleep, and the amātra (letterless fourth) to turīya [MAN912].

**In pañcakṛtya (the five acts of Śiva): S, Śiva Purāṇa and Śaiva Siddhānta** [P5K].

| | sṛṣṭi (creation) | sthiti (maintenance) | saṃhāra (dissolution) | tirobhāva (concealment) | anugraha (grace, release) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brahmā | ● | | | | |
| Viṣṇu | | ● | | | |
| Rudra | | | ● | | |
| (added) | | | | Maheśvara | Sadāśiva |

The three stay as they are. The two added acts are new deities stacked above them. The first four govern the world and the fifth frees the soul [P5K].

### 1c. Vyāhṛti (utterances: bhūḥ earth, bhuvaḥ the space between, svaḥ heaven)

**In four: S, Taittirīya 1.5.** The sage Māhācamasya taught a fourth utterance, mahaḥ (greatness). "That is brahman, that is the self (body). The other gods are its limbs" [TU15].

| | worlds | lights | Veda (scripture) | breaths |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bhūḥ | this world | agni (fire) | ṛc (verse) | prāṇa (in-breath) |
| bhuvaḥ | antarikṣa (mid-space) | vāyu (air) | sāman (chant) | apāna (down-breath) |
| svaḥ | the yonder world | āditya (sun) | yajus (formula) | vyāna (diffused breath) |
| **mahaḥ** | āditya (sun) | candramas (moon) | brahman | anna (food) |

**In the seven loka (worlds): S.** The three are the first three, followed by mahar, jana, tapas and satya [VP27].
**In the five prāṇa: S, partial.** Only prāṇa, apāna and vyāna are named. Samāna (middle breath) and udāna (up-breath) are left out, and the fourth slot goes to food instead [TU15]. **In oṃ: S.** The three are the "world-body" of a, u, m (Maitrī 6.5) [MU65].

### 1d. The pattern the sources share

| Source | the three | the fourth | how the fourth relates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taittirīya 1.5 [TU15] | bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, svaḥ | mahaḥ | the body, of which the three are limbs |
| Māṇḍūkya [MAN912] | waking, dream, sleep | turīya | the ground, not a further step |
| Ṛg Veda 1.164.45 [RV164] | three quarters of vāk (speech), "hidden in secret" | the spoken quarter | the reverse case: the fourth is the *outer* one |
| Bhartṛhari [VP159] | vaikharī, madhyamā, paśyantī | grammar is their "param padam" (highest station) | the ground was named parā only later |
| puruṣārtha [NWEP] | trivarga (the three worldly aims) | mokṣa | added later as release |
| Veda [VEDG] | trayī (the triple Veda) | Atharva | added later |

There are two kinds of fourth. One is a whole that holds the three: mahaḥ, turīya, parā. The other is a member added later as release: mokṣa, Atharva, anugraha. The three also sometimes *refuse* to distribute and sit inside a single member instead: the antaḥkaraṇa is all sattva, prāṇa is all rajas, and ahaṃkāra contains all three.

## 2. Splitting by load: how members come out of members

| Set | Rule of the split | Source |
|---|---|---|
| mahābhūta (elements) | **Emanation:** ākāśa, then vāyu, then agni, then āpas, then pṛthivī. Each comes out of the one before and adds one sense quality, from subtle to gross | Taittirīya 2.1 [TU21] |
| elements, mixed | **Trivṛtkaraṇa (making threefold):** fire, water and earth (called food) each take in the other two | Chāndogya 6.3 to 6.4 [CU63] |
| elements, mixed | **Pañcīkaraṇa (making fivefold):** each element keeps half of itself and takes one-eighth of each of the other four. It extends the threefold rule | Pañcadaśī 1.26 to 1.27 [PD1] [PK] |
| kośa (sheaths) | **Nesting:** each sheath is "filled by" the next one inward and has the same shape. The inner pervades the outer | Taittirīya 2.2 to 2.5 [TU21] |
| antaḥkaraṇa (inner organ) | **One organ named by function:** manas when weighing, buddhi when deciding, ahaṃkāra when claiming "mine", citta when seeking or remembering | Vivekacūḍāmaṇi 93 to 94 [VC93] |
| antaḥkaraṇa, counted | Sāṃkhya counts 3 (SK 33). Vedānta counts 4. The Vedāntasāra folds them back to 2, merging citta into buddhi and ahaṃkāra into manas. **Contested:** which pairs merge varies by text | [SK33] [VS] |
| prāṇa (breaths) | **Delegation:** "as an emperor commands his officers, 'rule these villages', so prāṇa disposes the other prāṇas, each in its place" | Praśna 3.4 [PRU3] |
| mind and breath | **One with many names:** desire, resolve, doubt and the rest "are all mind". Prāṇa, apāna, vyāna, udāna, samāna "are all prāṇa" | Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.5.3 [BU153] |
| ahaṃkāra (I-maker) | **One into three by guṇa.** The rajas form powers the other two | Sāṃkhyakārikā 25 [SK25] |
| vāk (speech) | **3, then 4:** Bhartṛhari has three levels. Somānanda's line and Abhinavagupta add parā (the supreme) above them | [VP159] [BHA] |
| trimūrti (three forms) | **3, then 5:** concealment and grace are added above the three | [P5K] |
| graha (planets) | **7 = 2 + 5:** the two luminaries, plus five planets that govern the five elements (Mars fire, Mercury earth, Jupiter space, Venus water, Saturn air). The two nodes then make 9 | BPHS 3.20 [BPHS3] |
| vāra (weekday) | **A fixed order read at a stride:** the hours run in Chaldean order, and 24 mod 7 = 3, so each day's ruler is three steps further along | [DIO37] [VARA] |
| nakṣatra (lunar mansions) | **27 = 9 lords × 3 passes:** each lord rules three mansions 120° apart. **108 pāda (quarters) = 12 signs × 9 navāṃśa (ninth-parts)** | [VIM] [PADA] |
| rāśi (signs) | **12 = 4 triplicities × 3 modalities** | Ptolemy, *Tetrabiblos* 1.18 [PTOL]; BPHS 4.5 [BPHS4] |

The split rules, abstracted. (1) Emanation: subtle to gross, each new member adding one quality. (2) Mixing: every member contains every other, its own share dominant. (3) Nesting: inner pervades outer. (4) Function: one organ, named by what it is doing. (5) Delegation: a principal appoints officers by region. (6) Adding a ground above, or a release after. (7) Striding: one cycle read at a fixed step.

## 3. Geometry of mind, thinking and communication

**The root triad, Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.5.3:** "Three he made for himself": manas (mind), vāk (speech) and prāṇa (breath, life). Each is then shown as one thing with many names [BU153].

**Antaḥkaraṇa (inner organ)** [VC93] [SK33]

| Member | English | Function | Sāṃkhya (3) | Vedāntasāra (2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| manas | mind | weighing pros and cons, coordinating the senses | ● | ● (takes in ahaṃkāra) |
| buddhi | intellect | determining, deciding | ● | ● (takes in citta) |
| ahaṃkāra | I-maker | claiming "mine" | ● | merged |
| citta | mind-stuff | memory, seeking | (not counted separately) | merged |

**Vāk (speech), in four levels** [VP159] [BHA] [RV164]

| Level | English | Bhartṛhari | Kashmir Śaiva śakti (power) | Avasthā (state) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parā | supreme | not named. **Contested:** some equate his śabda-brahman (word-absolute) with it | — | turīya (the fourth) |
| paśyantī | the seeing | ● undivided intuition, pratibhā (flash) | icchā (will) | suṣupti (deep sleep) |
| madhyamā | the middle | ● sequenced in buddhi (intellect), not yet sounded | jñāna (knowing) | svapna (dream) |
| vaikharī | the elaborated | ● articulated sound | kriyā (action) | jāgrat (waking) |

The śakti column comes from later Śaiva readers. The avasthā column is a modern correspondence, not found in Bhartṛhari: **contested** [LVS]. The Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa gives another four quarters of speech: mind, sight, hearing, speech [RV164].

**Grammar of communication (vyākaraṇa, analysis)** [KAR] [VERB] [PRAY] [VIBH]

| Division | Members (English) | Count |
|---|---|---|
| kāraka (roles in an action; Pāṇini 1.4.23 to 55) | kartṛ (agent), karman (object), karaṇa (instrument), sampradāna (recipient), apādāna (source), adhikaraṇa (locus) | 6 |
| puruṣa (person) | prathama "first" (he/she, the one spoken of), madhyama "middle" (you, the one addressed), uttama "last" (I, the speaker) | 3 |

The finite verb is a 3 × 3 grid: three persons by three numbers, 9 forms for each pada. The kāraka are the six ways a participant can stand toward one action, and the vibhakti are the endings that mark them.

## 4. Astrology's geometry

**Twelve bhāva (houses), grouped as four trikoṇa (trines) by puruṣārtha (aims)** [TRI] [BHV]. The rows are the four aims, and the columns are the first, second and third house of each trine. The first column is the four kendra (angles): 1, 4, 7 and 10 each open a different aim.

| | kendra (angle) | 2nd of the trine | 3rd of the trine |
|---|---|---|---|
| dharma (right order) | 1 tanu (self) | 5 putra (children, intelligence) | 9 dharma (teacher, fortune) |
| artha (means) | 10 karma (deeds, office) | 2 dhana (wealth, **speech**) | 6 ari (enemies, service) |
| kāma (desire) | 7 jāyā (partner) | 11 lābha (gains) | 3 sahaja (siblings, courage, effort) |
| mokṣa (release) | 4 sukha (home, happiness) | 8 āyus (longevity, the hidden) | 12 vyaya (loss, letting go) |

The 1-5-9 dharma trine is also called *the* trikoṇa (trine). The kendras are the four angles, so each aim starts from an angle.

**Signs: the three modes × the four elements** [BPHS4] [PTOL] [TRIP]

| | fire, pitta (bile) | earth, vāta (wind) | air, "mixed" | water, kapha (phlegm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cara (movable; Western cardinal) | Meṣa (Aries) | Makara (Capricorn) | Tulā (Libra) | Karka (Cancer) |
| sthira (fixed) | Siṃha (Leo) | Vṛṣabha (Taurus) | Kumbha (Aquarius) | Vṛścika (Scorpio) |
| dvisvabhāva (dual; Western mutable) | Dhanus (Sagittarius) | Kanyā (Virgo) | Mithuna (Gemini) | Mīna (Pisces) |

**Contested:** BPHS 4.5 groups the trines by doṣa (humour): bilious, windy, mixed, phlegmatic. It names elements only in the descriptions of single signs. The Western reading sees the four trines as four elements [BPHS4] [TRIP].

## 5. The flow's reading, not his

This section is a suggestion to react to. It is neither the psyche's word nor sourced doctrine. The base is the 3-Metaflow configuration from the first report: Brahmā is **making** (new capability), Viṣṇu is **keeping** (upkeep of what runs), and Śiva is **clearing** (reaping, retiring, hygiene). Each row below applies one rule from §2 when a line overloads.

| To | Line overloaded | Traditional rule (§2) | Resulting lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | none in particular. The three overload *each other* through coordination | add a whole above, like mahaḥ or turīya [TU15] [MAN912] | making, keeping, clearing, plus one **holding** line (psyche, vision, routing) that is "the body, the others limbs", not a peer |
| 6 (3 + 4 − 1) | keeping | one organ named by function [VC93] | keeping splits into **weighing** (manas: triage), **deciding** (buddhi: review), **owning** (ahaṃkāra: who holds what), **remembering** (citta: records) |
| 5 | clearing | pañcakṛtya: concealment and grace added [P5K] | making, keeping, clearing, **concealing** (archive, hold back, not yet released), **releasing** (delivery to the living, grace) |
| 7 (3 + 5 − 1) | keeping, by direction of work | prāṇa as an emperor's officers [PRU3] | keeping becomes inflow (prāṇa: intake), outflow (apāna: elimination), digestion (samāna: integration), circulation (vyāna: distribution), ascent (udāna: escalation) |
| 5 | all three evenly | pañcīkaraṇa: half its own, an eighth of each other [PD1] | five lines, each spending about half its effort on its own work and about an eighth on each of the others |
| 8 (3 + 6 − 1) | making | the kāraka of one action [KAR] | making splits by role: **agent** (does), **object** (what is made), **instrument** (tools), **recipient** (for whom), **source** (from what), **locus** (where it lives) |
| 7 | the whole set | graha, 7 = 2 + 5 [BPHS3] | two luminaries (Sun: psyche and vision; Moon: memory and records) plus five element lines. Rāhu and Ketu are optional for 9 (an obsession or intake line, a release line). Weekday order is a rota; Chaldean order is a precedence |
| 9 to 27 | any | nakṣatra: 9 lords × 3 passes [VIM] | each of 9 lines takes one task in each of the three phases (making, keeping, clearing) |

One choice decides which row applies. If the overload is *coordination* among the three, the fourth goes above them (mahaḥ, turīya). If one line is *too heavy*, that line splits by function (antaḥkaraṇa) or by direction (prāṇa). If the cycle lacks an *ending*, it gains acts after the three (pañcakṛtya).
