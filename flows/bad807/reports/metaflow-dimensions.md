# Metaflow dimensions: traditional sets of 3 to 7

Research report for flow bad807, delegated by the main flow. Read-only web research,
2026-10-04. Bracketed tags such as [BG14] point to `## Sources`. **Contested** marks a
claim on which sources disagree. Transliteration is IAST.

## Three

**Guṇa (the three strands of prakṛti).** Order: sattva, rajas, tamas [BG14].
- sattva: clarity, light, balance.
- rajas: activity, passion, drive.
- tamas: inertia, darkness, weight.
- Why this order: the Gītā lists them from finest to grossest. **Contested:** Maitrī Upaniṣad 5.2
  gives the order in which they *emerge* as the reverse: tamas, then rajas, then sattva [MU52].

**Trimūrti.** Order: Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva (Rudra).
- Brahmā: creation. Viṣṇu: preservation. Śiva: dissolution, clearing the ground for renewal.
- Why: the order of a cosmic cycle. Maitrī 5.2 maps rajas to Brahmā, sattva to Viṣṇu, tamas to Rudra [MU52].
- **Contested:** the trimūrti as a doctrine is Purāṇic and late. It is rarely a working cult, and some
  practitioners call the "Hindu trinity" a Western overreading [HT05].

**Vyāhṛti (the three great utterances).** Order: bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, svaḥ, which are earth, the space between, and heaven.
These are the first three of the sapta loka (see Seven) [VP27].

**Hermetic/Western.** Hermes is *trismegistos*, "thrice-greatest". Paracelsus's *tria prima*
are sal (body, what stays fixed), sulphur (soul, what burns), and mercurius (spirit, what is volatile) [OXTP].

## Four

**Puruṣārtha (the aims of a human life).** Order: dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa [NWEP].
- dharma: right order, duty. artha: means, wealth, power. kāma: desire, pleasure, aesthetic fulfilment.
  mokṣa: release from the round of births.
- Why: the first three (*trivarga*) are the worldly aims and are older. Mokṣa was added later, from
  Upaniṣadic renunciation. **Contested:** which aim ranks first. Dharma texts put dharma first.
  The Arthaśāstra (1.7) holds that artha is chief because the other two depend on it [NWEP].

**Varṇa.** Order: brāhmaṇa, rājanya/kṣatriya, vaiśya, śūdra. Rig Veda 10.90.12 derives them from
the mouth, arms, thighs and feet of Puruṣa [RV1090].
- brāhmaṇa: speech, priesthood. kṣatriya: rule, protection. vaiśya: production, trade. śūdra: service.
- Why: the descent of the cosmic body from head to foot. **Contested:** most scholars treat the Puruṣa
  Sūkta as a late layer of the Rig Veda, and whether varṇa was rigid or fluid is disputed.

**Āśrama (the stages of life).** Order: brahmacarya, gṛhastha, vānaprastha, saṃnyāsa [JABU] [ASR].
- brahmacarya: the student. gṛhastha: the householder. vānaprastha: withdrawal to the forest. saṃnyāsa: renunciation.
- Why: the order of a life. **Contested:** Olivelle argues the āśramas were first alternative lifelong
  paths and only later became a sequence. Jābāla 4 itself allows renouncing "from wherever one is" [ASR].

**Avasthā (states of consciousness, Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad).** Order: jāgrat, svapna, suṣupti, turīya [MANU].
- jāgrat: waking, turned outward. svapna: dream, turned inward. suṣupti: dreamless sleep, unified.
  turīya: "the fourth", the witness under the other three.
- Why: from outward toward ground. Turīya is not a further step but the ground of the other three.

**Veda.** Order: Ṛg, Yajur, Sāma, Atharva. Respectively: hymn, sacrificial formula, chant, and
practical/protective spells. **Contested:** the old canon is *trayī*, the three. The Atharva was admitted later.

**Western.** Empedocles' four roots are fire, air, water and earth, moved by Love and Strife [EMP].

## Five

**Mahābhūta (the gross elements).** Order of emanation in Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1: ākāśa, vāyu, agni
(tejas), āpas, pṛthivī [TU21].
- ākāśa: space or ether, which carries sound. vāyu: air, which carries touch. agni: fire, which carries form and sight.
  āpas: water, which carries taste. pṛthivī: earth, which carries smell.
- Why: each element comes out of the one before it and adds one more sense quality, from subtle to gross [TU21].

**Kośa (the sheaths of the self).** Order, outer to inner: annamaya, prāṇamaya, manomaya,
vijñānamaya, ānandamaya (Taittirīya 2.2 to 2.5) [TU21].
- anna: the food-body. prāṇa: the breath-body. manas: mind, sense-processing. vijñāna: discernment,
  understanding. ānanda: bliss, closest to the self.
- Why: each sheath is "filled by" the next one inward, and teaching moves inward [TU21].

**Prāṇa (the five vital winds).** Praśna Upaniṣad 3 [PRU3].
- prāṇa: the forward and inward breath, in eye, ear, mouth and nose. apāna: the downward breath, which eliminates.
  samāna: the middle breath, which digests and distributes. vyāna: the diffused breath, which circulates.
  udāna: the upward breath, which carries one out at death.
- **Contested order:** Praśna 3 introduces them as prāṇa, apāna, samāna, vyāna, udāna. Many later
  lists give prāṇa, apāna, vyāna, udāna, samāna.

**Western.** Aristotle adds aether, the *quinta essentia*, to the four elements. It is the matter of the heavens [EMP].

## Six

**Ṣaḍ-darśana (the six orthodox viewpoints).** These are conventionally paired [IEPH]:
- Nyāya (Gautama): logic, valid means of knowing. Vaiśeṣika (Kaṇāda): categories, atoms, what exists.
- Sāṃkhya (Kapila): enumeration of the tattvas, puruṣa and prakṛti. Yoga (Patañjali): discipline of mind.
- Pūrva Mīmāṃsā (Jaimini): exegesis of ritual action, dharma. Vedānta (Bādarāyaṇa): brahman, liberation.
- Why: the pairs move from how one knows, to what there is, to how one acts and what is ultimate. **Contested:**
  the "six" is a late doxographic convention, and the pairing is conceptual, not chronological [IEPH].

**Vedāṅga (the limbs of the Veda, Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.1.5).** Order: śikṣā, kalpa, vyākaraṇa, nirukta,
chandas, jyotiṣa [VEDG].
- śikṣā: phonetics. kalpa: ritual procedure. vyākaraṇa: grammar. nirukta: etymology. chandas: metre.
  jyotiṣa: timekeeping by the heavens.

**Ṣaṭ-cakra (the six cakras proper).** The source of the popular seven is titled "Explanation of the *six*
cakras". Sahasrāra is treated as beyond the system (see Seven) [WAL].

## Seven

**Cakra (Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa, Pūrṇānanda, 1577; Woodroffe, *The Serpent Power*, 1919).** Order, base
to crown [WAL]:
- mūlādhāra: root support, earth. svādhiṣṭhāna: one's own seat, water. maṇipūra: city of jewels, fire.
- anāhata: unstruck sound, air, the heart. viśuddha: purification, ether, the throat. ājñā: command, mind,
  between the brows. sahasrāra: thousand-petalled, beyond the elements.
- Why: kuṇḍalinī rises through them, and the five lower ones carry the five mahābhūtas in emanation order reversed.
  **Contested:** this is one system among many. Tantras teach 5, 6, 9, 21 and more cakras. The seven come
  from a 1577 treatise and the 13th-century Śāradā-tilaka, not from the Vedas. Modern colours come from
  Leadbeater (1927) [WAL].

**Saptarṣi (the seven seers).** Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.2.4 gives Gotama, Bharadvāja, Viśvāmitra, Jamadagni,
Vasiṣṭha, Kaśyapa, Atri, and maps them to the ears, eyes, nostrils and tongue [BRU224].
- Each is the founder of a gotra (clan line) and a Vedic family of hymns. Their order follows the organs of the head.
- **Contested:** the lists vary by text and by manvantara (world age). The Purāṇic list gives Marīci, Atri, Aṅgiras, Pulastya,
  Pulaha, Kratu, Vasiṣṭha.

**Sapta loka (the seven upper worlds).** Order, rising: bhūr, bhuvar, svar, mahar, jana, tapas,
satya [VP27].
- bhūr: earth. bhuvar: atmosphere, between earth and sun. svar: heaven, up to the pole star. mahar: the realm of
  sages that survives the lesser dissolution. jana: the sons of Brahmā. tapas: ascetic heat. satya: truth,
  Brahmā's world, not reborn.
- Why: rising in subtlety and in duration across the cosmic dissolutions [VP27].

**Graha by weekday (vāra).** Order: Sūrya/Ravi, Candra/Soma, Maṅgala, Budha, Guru/Bṛhaspati,
Śukra, Śani. The days are Ravivāra, Somavāra, Maṅgalavāra, Budhavāra, Guruvāra, Śukravāra, Śanivāra [BPHS3] [VARA].
- Sūrya: the Sun, soul, king. Candra: the Moon, mind, mother. Maṅgala: Mars, energy, courage, war.
  Budha: Mercury, speech, intellect, trade. Guru: Jupiter, wisdom, teacher, dharma.
  Śukra: Venus, love, art, comfort. Śani: Saturn, time, limit, discipline, grief.
- Why: each day is ruled by the planet of its first hour (horā). The hours cycle through the Chaldean order, and
  24 hours mod 7 = 3, so each next day's ruler is three steps further along that order [DIO37] [VARA].

**Chaldean order (the Hellenistic and Siddhāntic distance order).** Order, from outermost and slowest down to the Moon:
Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon (Śani, Guru, Maṅgala, Sūrya, Śukra, Budha, Candra) [DIO37] [VARA].
- Why: it ranks the planets by apparent speed, which the ancients read as distance. Cassius Dio 37.18-19 explains the
  weekday names by this order through the hours, and also through a musical tetrachord [DIO37].
- **Contested:** where the seven-day planetary week came from. The mainstream view is Babylonian and Hellenistic, adopted
  in India after about the 3rd century CE through the Yavanajātaka. A minority argues it originated in India [VARA] [YAV].

**Navagraha.** The seven, plus Rāhu (the ascending lunar node, "head", eclipse, obsession) and
Ketu (the descending node, "tail", detachment, release), listed in weekday order [BPHS3].
The nodes are shadow-grahas (chāyā-graha) with no day of their own.

**Hermetic: the seven zones of the Harmony (Poimandres, Corpus Hermeticum I.25).** On its ascent the soul
gives back one energy to each zone [POIM]:
1. growth and waning; 2. the device of evils; 3. the guile of desire; 4. domineering arrogance;
5. unholy daring and rashness; 6. striving for wealth by evil means; 7. ensnaring falsehood.
- **Inference, not in the text:** commentators read the zones as rising from the Moon in Chaldean order: Moon,
  Mercury, Venus, Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn. The text names zones, not planets [POIM].

**Hermetic/Renaissance correspondences (Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia*, 1533).** The planets and their metals
are Saturn and lead, Jupiter and tin, Mars and iron, the Sun and gold, Venus and copper, Mercury and quicksilver,
the Moon and silver. Agrippa lists them in Chaldean order [AGR].

**"Seven Hermetic principles" (*The Kybalion*, 1908).** Mentalism, Correspondence, Vibration, Polarity,
Rhythm, Cause and Effect, Gender [KYB].
- Mentalism: "the All is mind". Correspondence: "as above, so below". Vibration: all moves. Polarity: opposites
  are degrees of one thing. Rhythm: the swing of the pendulum. Cause and Effect: nothing is chance.
  Gender: the masculine and feminine principles in all things.
- **Contested:** this is modern. Scholars attribute it to the New Thought writer W. W. Atkinson, not to the ancient
  Hermetica. It is not part of the Vedic or Jyotiṣa material [KYB].

## The flow's reading, not his

This section is a suggestion to react to. None of it is the psyche's, and none of it is sourced doctrine.
Each line maps one long-running line of work (a Metaflow) onto one member.

**3, trimūrti or guṇa, in cycle order:**
- Brahmā / rajas: making. New capability, new repositories, new designs.
- Viṣṇu / sattva: keeping. Upkeep of what runs: nexuses, the operating system, deployed services.
- Śiva / tamas: clearing. Reaping, retiring, disk hygiene, closing what is done.

**4, puruṣārtha, dharma first:**
- dharma: the law of the system. Psyche, vision, skills, behavior.
- artha: means. Infrastructure, tooling, quota, money.
- kāma: what the living enjoys and uses. Presentations, books, the interfaces he touches.
- mokṣa: release. Autonomy and self-sufficiency, the private and open-source seat.

**5, kośa, outer to inner (the order of dependency):**
- anna: the substrate. Hardware, the OS, Nix.
- prāṇa: what breathes. Running nexuses, sockets, daemons.
- manas: sensing and routing. Messaging, flows, coordination.
- vijñāna: discernment. Review, testing, knowledge records.
- ānanda: the psyche itself. Vision, the living's words, their distillation.

**6, darśana, in pairs:**
- Nyāya: proof. Testing and verification.
- Vaiśeṣika: ontology. Types, data and their kinds.
- Sāṃkhya: enumeration. Catalogues, inventories, knowledge of what exists.
- Yoga: discipline. Agent behavior, effort, low-power practice.
- Mīmāṃsā: right procedure. Skills, operations, rituals of commit and publish.
- Vedānta: the ultimate aim. Vision and psyche.

**7, graha, read in two orders:**
- Sūrya: the self at the centre. Psyche and vision.
- Candra: mind and memory. Transcripts, records, recall.
- Maṅgala: force and defence. Testing, security, breaking upgrades.
- Budha: speech and exchange. Messaging, wire protocols, languages.
- Guru: teaching. Skills curriculum, documentation.
- Śukra: beauty and delight. Presentations, books, artifacts for the living.
- Śani: time and limits. Quota, reaping, hygiene, succession.
- In weekday order (Sun to Saturn) this is a rotation for attention, one line per day. In Chaldean order
  (Saturn to Moon) it is a hierarchy from the slowest and most constraining line down to the fastest and most
  responsive one. The two orders imply different things: a schedule versus a precedence.
