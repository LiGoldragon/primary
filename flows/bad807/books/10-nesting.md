**Contested**: the sources disagree. Everything up to the last section is sourced research.

## 1. The three inside the four and five

The same shape recurs: three, then a fourth that is either the whole holding them, or a member added after them.

| Source | The three | The fourth (or fifth) | How it relates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.5 | bhūḥ (earth), bhuvaḥ (the space between), svaḥ (heaven) | mahaḥ (greatness) | the body; the three are its limbs |
| Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad | jāgrat (waking), svapna (dream), suṣupti (deep sleep) | turīya (the fourth) | the ground, not a further step |
| Ṛg Veda 1.164.45 | three quarters of vāk (speech), "hidden in secret" | the spoken quarter | reversed: the fourth is the outer one |
| Bhartṛhari | vaikharī, madhyamā, paśyantī (levels of speech) | parā (the supreme) | named only by later readers |
| puruṣārtha (aims of life) | trivarga (the three worldly aims) | mokṣa (release) | added later, as release |
| Veda (scripture) | trayī (the triple Veda) | Atharva | added later |
| pañcakṛtya (five acts of Śiva) | creation, maintenance, dissolution | concealment, grace | two acts stacked above the three |

**In avasthā (states): R.** Chained from Maitrī 5.2 and the Parabrahma Upaniṣad: rajas to waking, sattva to dream, tamas to deep sleep; turīya beyond the guṇa.
**In puruṣārtha (aims): R only.** Dharma to sattva, artha to rajas, kāma to rajas and tamas, mokṣa beyond. No source maps them.

**Where the three refuse to spread: S.** The whole antaḥkaraṇa (inner organ) is made of sattva; all five prāṇa (vital winds) of rajas (Pañcadaśī 1.20, 1.22).
The three appear one level up, inside ahaṃkāra (I-maker): sattvic gives the eleven organs, tamasic the subtle elements, rajasic powers both (Sāṃkhyakārikā 25).

## 3. Splitting by load

How tradition brings members out of members.

| Set | Rule of the split | Source |
|---|---|---|
| mahābhūta (elements) | **Emanation:** space, air, fire, water, earth; each from the one before, adding one sense quality | Taittirīya 2.1 |
| elements, mixed | **Trivṛtkaraṇa (making threefold):** fire, water, earth each take in the other two | Chāndogya 6.3 to 6.4 |
| elements, mixed | **Pañcīkaraṇa (making fivefold):** each keeps half itself, takes an eighth of each other | Pañcadaśī 1.26 to 1.27 |
| kośa (sheaths) | **Nesting:** each sheath is filled by the next inward; the inner pervades the outer | Taittirīya 2.2 to 2.5 |
| antaḥkaraṇa (inner organ) | **One organ named by function:** manas weighing, buddhi deciding, ahaṃkāra claiming "mine", citta remembering | Vivekacūḍāmaṇi 93 to 94 |
| antaḥkaraṇa, counted | Sāṃkhya counts 3, Vedānta 4, Vedāntasāra folds to 2. **Contested:** which pairs merge | Sāṃkhyakārikā 33; Vedāntasāra |
| prāṇa (breaths) | **Delegation:** as an emperor appoints officers to villages, prāṇa places the other breaths | Praśna 3.4 |
| mind and breath | **One with many names:** desire, resolve, doubt "are all mind"; the five breaths "are all prāṇa" | Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.5.3 |
| vāk (speech) | **3, then 4:** parā (the supreme) added above Bhartṛhari's three | Abhinavagupta's line |
| trimūrti (three forms) | **3, then 5:** concealment and grace added above | Śiva Purāṇa |
| graha (planets) | **7 = 2 + 5:** two luminaries plus five element-planets; the two nodes make 9 | BPHS 3.20 |
| vāra (weekday) | **Stride:** hours in Chaldean order; 24 mod 7 = 3, so each day's ruler is three steps on | Dio Cassius 37 |
| nakṣatra (lunar mansions) | **27 = 9 lords × 3 passes**, 120° apart; **108 pāda (quarters) = 12 signs × 9** | Viṃśottarī |
| rāśi (signs) | **12 = 4 triplicities × 3 modalities** | Ptolemy; BPHS 4.5 |

One line, overloaded, divides by function; the four names are the tradition's own for one inner organ.

The rules, abstracted:
- Emanation: subtle to gross, each new member adds one quality.
- Mixing: every member holds every other, its own share chief.
- Nesting: the inner pervades the outer.
- Function: one organ, named by what it does.
- Delegation: a principal appoints officers by region.
- Adding: a ground above, or a release after.
- Striding: one cycle read at a fixed step.

## 4. Mind and speech

**The root triad: S.** "Three he made for himself": manas (mind), vāk (speech), prāṇa (breath). Each is one thing with many names (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.5.3).

| Member | English | Function | Sāṃkhya (3) | Vedāntasāra (2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| manas | mind | weighing, coordinating the senses | ● | ● (takes in ahaṃkāra) |
| buddhi | intellect | determining, deciding | ● | ● (takes in citta) |
| ahaṃkāra | I-maker | claiming "mine" | ● | merged |
| citta | mind-stuff | memory, seeking | not counted apart | merged |

**Vāk (speech), four levels**

| Level | English | Bhartṛhari | Śaiva śakti (power) | avasthā (state) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parā | the supreme | not named; **contested** | — | turīya (the fourth) |
| paśyantī | the seeing | undivided flash, pratibhā (intuition) | icchā (will) | suṣupti (deep sleep) |
| madhyamā | the middle | sequenced in buddhi, not yet sounded | jñāna (knowing) | svapna (dream) |
| vaikharī | the elaborated | articulated sound | kriyā (action) | jāgrat (waking) |

The śakti column is from later Śaiva readers. The avasthā column is a modern correspondence: **contested**.
