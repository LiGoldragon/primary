## 1. The floor: three Metaflows that never close

The flow's proposal. The three acts of the trimūrti (three forms), each carried by its guṇa (strand) and by one graha (planet).

```
Line       Graha (planet)       Act (trimūrti)          Guṇa (strand)          Holds
keeping    Candra (Moon)        sthiti (maintenance)    sattva (clarity)       what runs: nexuses, the OS, records
making     Budha (Mercury)      sṛṣṭi (creation)        rajas (activity)       designs, code, protocols, new repositories
clearing   Śani (Saturn)        saṃhāra (dissolution)   tamas (inertia)        reaping, retiring, hygiene, quota, succession
```

### Why this triad
The acts are a cycle of work: what is made is kept, and what is kept is in the end cleared. Every line of work falls in one.
The guṇa tie lets the seven inherit: BPHS (Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra, the classic text on the graha) 3.22 spreads the three guṇa across the seven graha (**S**), the one set in the research where tradition does this.
Pañcakṛtya (Śiva's five acts) adds acts above the three and leaves the three intact (**S**): the floor survives expansion.

### Why these three graha
Budha is "the clever giver of language" (BPHS 3.12, **S**); Śani is time and limit; Candra is mind and memory, the fastest of the seven.
The three are voices in his sense: Metaflows with no known ending. The four that open later are Metaflows that end.

**Contested.** The trimūrti as a doctrine is late and Purāṇic; some call the "Hindu trinity" a Western overreading.
The guṇa order is contested too: the Bhagavad Gītā lists sattva, rajas, tamas; the Maitrī Upaniṣad (a teaching text) 5.2 has them emerge in reverse.

### Why Sūrya opens first, by default
In every set the research found, the first overload of three is among the three themselves.
The fourth then comes as the whole that holds them: mahaḥ (greatness) over bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, svaḥ (earth, air, heaven); turīya (the fourth) under waking, dream, sleep.
Sūrya, the self at the centre (BPHS 3), holds the psyche and routes to the living. The order of the other three is the flow's guess: ruling 2.

**Contested.** The planetary week's origin: mainstream Babylonian and Hellenistic, a minority argues India.
The graha meanings (Sun the soul, Venus delight, Saturn limit) are BPHS 3; which line of work each takes is *R*, the flow's reading.

## 3. Contraction: a line folds back into its parent

The flow's proposal.

```
When      its demand stays under the contraction threshold (section 4)
Folds to  its parent in the lattice: Sūrya and Guru to Candra, Maṅgala to Śani, Śukra to Budha
Moves     its topics, its queued messages and its open proposals go to the parent
Its flow  receives no new work; it finishes its turn and is reaped as any idle seat is
Never     a floor line: Candra, Budha and Śani do not fold; three is the floor
Counted   a folded line's demand is still read on its topics, so it can open again
```

## 4. Demand and availability

### What is counted
The flow's proposal. Every count is read per graha, on the topics the lattice gives it, even while it is folded into its parent.

```
Demand        queued messages addressed to its topics            needs a per-topic counter
              living records published on its topics             needs a per-topic counter
              open proposals awaiting his ruling                 read from the rulings files
              its flows past the changeover share                needs the context share of each flow
Pressure      messages + records + 2 × proposals + full flows    arithmetic, in code
Availability  quota left against the rhythm, per stack           due left = 1 − day/7, his "about 14% per day"
              a seat free                                        seat ceiling − live flows, from Flow's List
```

The quota rhythm and the context signal are his. Which inputs exist today: section 8.

### The rule
The flow's defaults: every number below (8, 2, 3 readings, 6 readings, seven seats) is the flow's guess, to be tuned. Ruling 3.

```
Expand    pressure ≥ 8 at 3 readings in a row, and quota left ≥ due, and a seat free
Hold      pressure 3 to 7: nothing changes
Contract  pressure ≤ 2 at 6 readings in a row, once open at least 6 readings
          or quota left < due: the open line with the least pressure folds
Limits    one change per reading; never under three, never over seven
Reading   taken at an event: a turn end, an enqueue, a record published, a ruling; never on a timer
```

### Why it does not flap
Hysteresis comes from three things: the gap between 8 and 2, the run of readings each side needs, and the six-reading minimum span.
Ties in demand open in the lattice order: Sūrya, Maṅgala, Śukra, Guru.

## 5. Clusters

The flow's proposal. A cluster is a set of Metaflows scoped to one situation or goal: it has a purpose and an end condition, it spans, and it ends.
For the cluster the flow offers gaṇa (troop, a band in attendance); the word is not from the research and is his to take or leave.

```
Opened by   a ruling of his, or a goal a seat sets
Size        3, then 5, then 12, by the same expand and contract rules, under a ceiling of its own
Twelve      offered as 4 × 3, like the rāśi (signs): four triplicities × three modes,
            cara (movable), sthira (fixed), dvisvabhāva (dual)  (Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos 1.18; BPHS 4.5, S)
Members     named by the cluster: his "aspect, situation, problem, implementation, testing"
Home        the floor or lattice line that receives its leftovers when it ends
Ended       when its end condition is judged met; members fold into Home, their flows reaped
```

**Contested.** BPHS 4.5 groups the triplicities by doṣa (humour), not by element; the Western reading uses elements.
The flow assigns no symbolism to the five.
