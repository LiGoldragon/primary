1. Vision/meaning.md (new). The meaning language is a logographic computer language whose signs are typed structs and enums forming one ontology held as a datom graph, defined in Ethos and backed by Rust. Its roots are the seven Vaiśeṣika categories; its verbs follow Pāṇini. A statement's subparts are reached by content-addressed link: the checksum over a piece's content and its links. A changed meaning has a new identity, so an annotation on the old meaning still points at the old meaning and its validity is judged anew. Linked pieces are kept by virtue of the link and released when the last link goes, as Nix keeps a store path while something references it. A complete statement is stored content-addressed at its root; a series of responses is a vector. Every Sanskrit-rooted type carries an English name, which may be a PascalCase sentence expression. Until the verb set lands, statements stay short prose, and a reader who lacks a block's spec treats it as an opaque string. Corrects Vision/datom.md (Meaning postponed).

The requester holds only a request ID, by which it asks for status or detail, messages the live subflow, and receives a message on completion if it is still the flow in charge.
