What we know, what is held, and what needs deciding

Several useful pieces are ready. The hard part is putting them together without treating a build, a service plan, or an old deployment record as if it were the machine running now.

What is working

Zeus’s system update and both Home repairs are complete. Current contact was verified at its observation point; that does not turn later time into continuous reachability.

The passive USB-downlink observer has passed its source and virtual-machine checks. Its lasting delivery belongs to the ordinary reviewed Ouranos composition repair. A transient observer witness is separate: runtime-only evidence, never Zeus deployment evidence.

Messenger and Claude completed through a corrected Home generation: managed messenger bindings were repaired and Claude Code 2.1.284 was included. This is not a claim that every service on every machine is healthy.

Codex has staged stable and Next builds, but they are unactivated. The authenticated catalog checked in this work exposed gpt-6-sol, not a gpt-6.1-sol slug. That is an account/catalog observation, not evidence about every possible model version.

Why Ouranos is still hard to change

Ouranos has four different references that must not be collapsed: the system running now; the system selected for the next boot or profile; the historical orchestration record; and the newly built observer candidate.

The candidate is a deliberately lean BaseHost build. It leaves Home out by design, so its lack of Home is expected. It proves the observer is in the closure. It does not prove switching the live machine is safe.

A Field dry activation finished, but its plan would stop the user Home service and other unrelated services. A prior recovery attempt also disrupted networking and Home before failing. Neither result supplies a proven way back.

The dry activation made runtime secret-management metadata. An empty later generation remains, while the temporary keyring and dry lists were reported removed. No secret content was read. This is a runtime-write observation, not proof that the dry activation had no effects.

What the cable evidence actually says

At 18:05–18:06 on 29 September, Field Sol passively observed the USB-facing NIC and bridge on Ouranos administratively up but without carrier; the bridge had no learned peers and its only slave was disabled. A DHCP acknowledgement on that link was recorded, but it was not confirmed as Zeus.

Before that later outage, Zeus recorded a USB-controller resume reinitialization at 15:46:45, then wired 1 Gbit/s carrier and its 10.44.0.10 address at 15:48:54–56. This records a separate recovery sequence, not a cause.

Field then correlated journals from both hosts. Ouranos lost carrier at 17:01:17 as Zeus’s wired NIC went down; recovery at 21:07:10–14 on Ouranos matches Zeus’s brief 10 Mbit/s rise, fall, and stable 1 Gbit/s carrier. Field’s summary records Kea assigning Zeus’s wired MAC a 10.44.0.10 lease at 21:07:15, followed by Yggdrasil events on both hosts. This corroborates a roughly four-hour carrier outage and automatic recovery after carrier. It does not identify the cause.

Overnight reachability remains unknown: uptime shows no reboot, and a later SSH or lease only proves contact at its own time. Cable, adapter, port, PHY, power, and negotiation remain possible; the xHCI message is not causal proof. A future watch must name its interval and leave a follow-up or a clear notice when current reachability is unknown.

What is temporary and what should last

A transient observer witness is temporary. It must disappear at reboot, create no durable unit or link, and record its own start, end, executable provenance, and events. Its lasting replacement belongs in the reviewed Ouranos composition, not in a parallel lifecycle.

The lasting direction is one reviewed Ouranos composition including Home. It must account for every intentional unit and service change, preserve the existing network owners, and have a target-proven recovery path. There is no approved parallel durable observer lifecycle.

Codex remains on hold. A Home-inclusive composition can declare managed Codex services for eligible users, so the final unit-delta review must show whether stable or Next endpoints would be touched. Separate state directories do not make a shared endpoint harmless.

The architecture we are aiming for

Ethos defines contract types. Datom carries the values that fill them. Signal is the protocol boundary, whose final role is still unsettled. Nexus is the process that holds a whole live concern together; it is not settled whether it is a foundation or a later composition over the existing stack.

The legacy Mind stack has not been migrated into that shape. Recent compensation work integrates specific repairs; it does not turn those repairs into durable definitions by itself. JSON and EDN can bridge data only through explicit mappings. The Clojure-versus-Nexus choice remains open.
