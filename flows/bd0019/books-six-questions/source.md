## Six questions on sessions, re-asked

1. **Starting.** Live seats do not start through the Flow nexus: two hand-run launcher scripts start them, Flow's own Start was refused each of the three times it was tried, and the Codex seat's title is set afterward through what looks like the wrong socket for the newest harness. Both launchers poll the transcript once a second to know the seat is up, and every brief is a file a person writes. Your words settle the shape: one fat first prompt, "no argument to give except a small description of the goal", creation "with almost zero LLM calls". The re-asked question: Flow starts every seat, from a typed launch request that names aspect, model, power and a goal, and composes the prompt itself from the concentrated vision; the launcher scripts are deleted, not kept beside it.

2. **Messaging and hooks.** One hook exists on each harness, at session start, telling Herdr the pane; nothing fires at a reply's end, at exit, or at retirement. A message the messenger cannot deliver is held and never retried by anything; Flow and the messenger keep two registries that do not know each other, so a closed pane leaves a live route behind. You settled "avoid polling ... hook-based" and "Nobody should be checking anything repeatedly." The re-asked question: Flow is the one registry, every harness event reaches it through a hook, and the messenger delivers from Flow's state, holding nothing it cannot deliver on the next event.

3. **Deprecation and restart.** Retiring a seat marks a row and leaves the pane open; stopping closes the pane and leaves the route; the reaper is dry-run only; indexes are months stale; two thousand archived Codex sessions sit in two homes. Flow's Restart always refuses, and a successor is in truth a new seat started by hand with its predecessor retired by delegation. You settled that a replaced seat is reaped and archived "so it doesn't keep getting messages", and that restart carries concentrated vision forward. The re-asked question: one act, succession, owned by Flow: it starts the successor with the concentrated brief, moves the route, retires the predecessor and archives its transcript, with nothing left behind; and whether that act runs on its own when a context is old, or only on your word.

4. **Ephemeral sessions.** Subflows, Codex workers and the one ephemeral exec register nowhere; they are tracked, when at all, by receipt files. Your words are open here, and in tension: subflows "just use their parents" (31 August) against a subflow "independent and can reply to a successor" (19 September); and "subagents are a type of skill ... implemented by a fresh flow" (1 October). The re-asked question: an ephemeral session is a Flow object like any seat, with a parent and a lifetime, so a reply can find its parent's successor; and whether that makes subflows independent flows, which your 31 August word denied.

## Rulings

- Succession automatic on old context, or on your word.
- Ephemeral sessions as Flow objects with a parent; and the 31 August against 19 September tension.
- Handoff: your 21 August "imposing the old opinion on the new flow is the wrong approach" against 1 October's concentrated vision passed forward; I read the later word as the ruling and the earlier as a warning against opinion rather than against vision.
