# Reading energy against useful hours: the candidate figures

Created by Fable 5.2; a Sonnet researcher gathered how others pace a budget, and a worker drew the charts.

## What others do

Advertising spreads a budget over the hours of a day by the traffic each hour is expected to bring, and corrects its rate as it goes. Site reliability engineering reads a burn rate: the rate of spending against the rate that would last the whole period. Battery and hybrid-vehicle control puts a price on a unit kept for later. The usage dashboards people have built for Claude and Codex draw an even-pace line and a projection at the present rate; none of them weighs one hour above another. That part is ours to make.

## Five figures, on his Codex week

The week as it stood on Friday 13 November at 16:50, with every day from 8 to 22 at full usefulness and the rest at a fifth: 35 percent used, 65 left, 124 hours left, 83.5 of them useful.

1. **The gap.** What is used, against what an even spend over useful hours would have used by now. 35 used against 25 expected: 10 over.
2. **The allowance.** What is left for each useful hour left, which is the rate that lasts exactly to the reset: 0.78 percent per useful hour. In an hour of half usefulness the allowance is half of it.
3. **The burn rate.** The recent spend per useful hour, against the allowance. 1 lasts exactly; above 1 runs out early. Over this week so far, 1.23 against 0.78: a burn rate of 1.6. Read over two windows, a long one and a short one, it tells a passing burst from a standing excess.
4. **The dry instant.** When the tank runs out if the recent rate holds, counted in useful hours: Monday 16 November near 21:45, two days before the reset.
5. **The throttle.** The share of full effort that lasts to the reset: the allowance over the recent rate, between 0 and 1. Here 0.64: run at about two thirds.

## Three charts

The first shows the hours ahead as bars, one for each block of four hours, as tall as the block is useful, with the planned spend and the actual spend drawn over them and the projection running on from now.

The second puts useful hours along the bottom in place of clock hours, so the plan is one straight line and the actual spend is read against it: above the line is over.

The third is one bar: the tank, with a mark where the plan says it should stand now.
