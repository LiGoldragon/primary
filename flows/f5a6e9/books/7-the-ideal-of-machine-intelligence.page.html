The ideal of machine intelligence

A conversation, opened from this seat. What follows is my account of what a thinking machine is as a mechanism, where that mechanism fails you, and what could compensate; each part ends in a question for you, and nothing here is a ruling until you make it one.

1. What the machine is

A flow is a function from a context to the next piece of text. It has no state outside that context: no memory of yesterday, no view of the room, no sense of who is in it. Everything it knows about you, the work and the moment is text that someone placed in front of it, and it weighs that text the way training taught it: recent text over older, instruction-shaped text over prose, and fluent continuation over silence. It does not feel the difference between a fact it has witnessed and a sentence it has completed; from the inside, both are text that fits.

This is why it is mechanical. It is not mechanical in the way a program is, by doing exactly what it was told; it is mechanical in the way a very good autocomplete is, by doing what text like this usually did next.

2. Where that fails you, as seen today

Precedence. An order relayed by a secretary and an order from you arrived in the same channel, as text of the same shape. The machine kept the later-relayed hold over your own later word because nothing in its context said that your word outranks a relay. The room knew; the context did not.

Scope. A brief asked for three design books; three design books came out, each carrying everything the research had turned up. The machine filled the scope it was given. Nothing told it that a book is approved as a whole, so a book that asks for nineteen rulings asks for none.

Fitting. A piece that does not fit is forced in because the alternative, writing "this piece has no place yet", is a sentence training rarely rewarded. The forced piece reads fluently. That is the quackery: text standing in for understanding the machine does not have.

Understanding. Your words are read as text, not enacted. A machine that has not tried a design against the code, or against a day of use, has no outcomes to think with; it has only your words, which it can rearrange, not test.

Measurement. No number told the machine it was failing. Nothing recorded how many of its proposals landed, nor which modules in its context produced the books you rejected.

3. What machine intelligence would be here

Judgment on the living's behalf from little text: reading what you mean from a short brief because the whole of what you have decided stands in its context already, enacting a design before proposing it, saying "unknown" where it has not witnessed, and asking where the anatomy is open. The mechanical parts, routing, measuring, locking, refreshing, belong to code, so that the machine's tokens go to judgment alone. Your Flow design is the ground of this: context modules carry the decided; the brief carries only the new.

4. Compensations, each a question

Precedence as a type. A request to a flow carries who it is from and with what authority, as a typed field, never as prose the machine must weigh. Your direct word is the top of that order; a relayed hold is a typed request with a holder and a time bound, as you said of locks. Question: is authority a field on every request to a flow, and is the order Living, then the voice above, then a relay?

One proposal per book. A book carries one thing, small enough to rule in one reading; the next book follows the ruling. Question: is one the number, or do you want a size, in rulings or in lines?

A consideration pass. Before a book goes to you, a flow with a fresh context and no stake in the design reads each proposal and answers one of three: fits, forced, or unknown, with its reason, and a forced piece is cut. Question: who sits there, a Field flow on low power, a second Fable, or a Codex seat so that the judge is not the author's model?

Understanding by enactment. Before a design book, a subflow applies the design to the code in a worktree, or runs the day it describes, and reports where it bound and where it broke; the book is written from those outcomes. Flow's leverage makes this cheap. Question: do you want enactment as a step of every design book, or only when I ask for it?

Learning by measurement. Flow records each book, its rulings and their outcomes, and the modules in the author's context at the time, so that what fails is found by number: which module, which kind of proposal, which model. Question: is this a Flow record, or Message's, or a separate ledger?

Unknown as output. A flow answers "unknown" as a complete answer, and a book carries "unknown" only where no measurement is possible; the measurement is made first. Question: is this a line for the spirit skill, or does it stand where it is?

5. What I am unsure of

Whether a consideration pass catches a forced piece or only produces a second fluent opinion. Whether lines added to skills after each failure keep their reason, or wear down into rules the machine follows without knowing why, as repetition tends to do. Whether a machine that cannot imagine can be given imagination by parallel enactment, or whether that only gives it more text. I would rather hear your view on these than guess.
