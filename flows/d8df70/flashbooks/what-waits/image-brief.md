The living wants illustrations that pull them in: rich, elaborate, fully
painted images an image model makes directly. Not diagrams, not flat vector
art, not SVG-like shapes.

Shared style for all eight, so they read as one book: a hand-painted isometric
cutaway in ink and gouache on warm cream paper. Lamplit, warm amber light
against cool blue-grey shadow, with one recurring accent: a fat, bright red,
hand-coloured line or thread. Dense organic detail that rewards looking, a
storybook-archive mood, quiet and a little mysterious. Portrait 2:3. NO text,
letters, numbers, captions, labels, logos, or signage anywhere in the image;
where a card, tag or sheet appears, it is blank or shows illegible
squiggles. No people's faces in close-up; figures are small, seen from behind
or at a distance. Nothing that looks like marketing.

01. The registry hall. A lamplit hall with a round central desk: green-shaded
lamp, inkwell, blank answer sheet, empty chair. Six alcoves around it, each
with a sealed scroll and an empty waiting chair: a clerk's desk with an
unsigned name card; a laptop cabled to a dark machine; a cellar stair down to
humming machines; a chest with five padlocks; a post-room tray; the mouth of a
corridor of lit doors. Worn floor paths run from every alcove to the centre
desk. One path is a fat red hand-coloured path, and a scroll travels along it
toward the desk.

02. The unsigned name card. A newly seated clerk, seen from behind, at a desk
whose name card is written but unsigned. A tray of wax-sealed letters is held
beside the desk under a small weight, waiting. A taut red thread runs across
the room from this desk to an older, dustier desk, where an old brass address
plate still hangs.

03. The washed-out road. In the foreground, a lit laptop on a workbench, with a
short cable to a dark tower machine. A hand holding a key shows that the near
door to the tower is open. Above, an elevated road that should carry carts of
parts to a distant workshop has washed out in the middle and ends in mist.
Wooden crates of unbuilt parts sit at the laptop's feet, and a second
workbench beside them is roped off.

04. The cellar of untagged machines. A vaulted stone cellar. Three brass and
iron machines hum, their lamps lit, each with a paper tag tied on that is
blank. A fourth machine stands cold and dark. On its intake tray lies one
sheet, crossed through with a red mark and returned.

05. The chest with five padlocks. A heavy iron-bound chest with five padlocks
of different shapes. Behind it, two empty chairs with cloaks still over their
backs. On one side, a keeper in teal holds open, empty hands. On the other, a
keeper in sage-green holds bolt cutters lowered, waiting.

06. The post room tray. A long wooden sorting tray in a post room. At the left,
paper slips curl and yellow with age, dust on them. At the right, wax-sealed
letters and ribbon-tied bundles sit crisp and untouched by time. On a
separate desk in front, one letter lies opened but unread, and a clerk's
hand hovers above it.

07. The corridor of lit doors. A long corridor of identical doors, receding.
Warm lamplight still glows through the gaps under several doors. One door
near the front swings shut by itself as its light goes out, trailing a thin
red thread that is being drawn in. Further along, a small figure with a
lantern stands at a door, head tilted, as if asking.

08. The desk, answered. The same round central desk as image 01, now with
six scrolls unrolled and held open by stones and the inkwell. The lamp is
bright. The six paths leading out to the alcoves glow softly, and small
scrolls travel outward along them, answers going home.
