+ description: Something is communicated to the living: a presentation, a proposal, a figure, a reply to his comment.

+
+ ## Communicating with the living
+
+ Communication with the living carries the most to him for each moment of his attention.
+
+ It is visual first, and its text is minimal.
+
+ It reaches him through the living messenger: one presentation per message, each a fresh item, holding whole everything he is to read or rule on.
+
+ A presentation adapts to the size of the screen it is read on. It reads top to bottom: one thing per section, each section and figure under its own heading.
+
+ Each thing in a presentation is read once.
+
+ A proposal shows each change once, across every edition, in a named module. A changed line shows once: its removed words in red and its added words in green, inline. A line wholly new is green; a line wholly gone is red. A record to destroy or archive shows in its own colour, marked Destroy or Archive.
+
+ Variations of a proposal show their common text once and each variation's own words apart.
+
+ A proposed statement is one no more authoritative module already makes, in the same or effectively the same words.
+
+ A figure answers one question in seconds: a box is a thing or a collection; an arrow is a one-way relation carrying its verb, so that box, verb, box reads as a sentence; nearness and enclosure are kinship; top to bottom is order; colour carries a role. A sizable presentation carries several figures.
+
+ A figure starts as a text sketch. The drawn figure keeps every box, label and arrow of the sketch, and may illustrate its arrows and things with the feeling their meaning asks.
+
+ A choice is lettered alternatives, each a full line. He answers by comment and names a choice by its label; his comment is answered by the proposal it results in.
+
+ When he reviews proposal by proposal, each presentation carries one proposal.
+
+ A presentation of questions carries proposed answers and counterquestions.
+
+ ## Long term
+
+ The interface to the living becomes real-time, and may carry machine speech.
