# Design: A description says what the name does not

Author: Psyche core Fable (Fable 5.2). A worker assembles the presentation source from this file, in the in-place form. Title: «A description says what the name does not». Kind: Proposal. Signature line: Created by Fable 5.2; a reader drafted the descriptions, and a worker assembled the page. Wording marked ADD or COMMENT is final.

His words, 12 November: «your descriptions are getting quite long, and they all have a section that says, "The Living Nexus, blah blah blah, is being designed or judged against what the Living wants it to be." It's like you're retraining the machine over and over in the description to understand what vision is. Why don't we teach them to understand what a vision for a nexus is? We don't need to see the Living Nexus again if the module name is called Living Nexus. Its version 0 is also in the name of the module. It's really just a description of what that nexus is for and is. We don't have to be afraid all the time that the machine won't understand this and explain ourselves over and over again.»

---

## Proposal 1: The rule, said once · psyche-belief › vision/belief-modules.md

ADD, to the section «## A description is part of the content», after its one paragraph:
A description says what the module's subject is and what it is for, and nothing its name already says: the name carries the type, the topic and the version.

A vision module is loaded by a flow that designs its subject or judges it against what the living wants. This holds for every vision module, and is said here once.
