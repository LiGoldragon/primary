# Third model: bootstrapping the big open-source pairing

Research for flow 024bc7, answering the psyche's 2026-09-13 statement (`vision/thirdModel.md`): pick an open-weight model and API provider for a third paired session beside Claude and Codex, judged on privacy/security goodwill and on doubting, conceptual, epistemological reasoning quality — plus a small same-family sibling runnable on Prometheus (AMD Strix Halo, 128GB unified memory, llama.cpp, currently running Gemma 4).

**Sourcing note.** All findings below came from WebSearch result snippets gathered today, 2026-09-13; I did not fetch full primary pages via WebFetch except where a search tool itself quoted primary text (license files, privacy-policy pages). Where a number or clause is load-bearing, treat it as approximate pending a direct read of the cited URL. Citations are numbered; the map is at the bottom. Passages marked **Judgment** are mine, not sourced.

## 2. Providers, judged on privacy/security goodwill

A key structural point (**Judgment**, not sourced): because all the models above ship as open weights, the lab's own hosted API is *not* the only way to reach the big model — a neutral third-party inference host can serve the same weights under its own, often stronger, privacy terms. This decouples "which country trained the model" from "who sees your prompts," which matters a lot for the Chinese-lab models below.

- **Together AI** — Zero Data Retention available; does not store inputs/outputs by default; training on data is opt-in only, revocable; SOC 2 Type II completed [30][31][32].
- **Fireworks AI** — Zero data retention is the *default* for serverless open-model inference, not a paid add-on; TLS 1.2+/AES-256; SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, ISO 27001, ISO 27701, ISO 42001 [33][34][35].
- **Groq** — Does not retain inference data or train on inputs/outputs by default; troubleshooting/abuse logs capped at 30 days; Zero Data Retention is now self-serve for every customer via the console, not just enterprise contracts [36][37].
- **DeepInfra** — Zero-retention policy stated; does not train on submitted data except for pass-through Google/Anthropic-hosted models (irrelevant to the models above, which DeepInfra hosts itself); SOC 2 and ISO 27001 certified [38][39].
- **OpenRouter** — Itself keeps no prompt/response content unless the customer opts into logging (which grants OpenRouter an irrevocable commercial-use right over logged content — a real caveat); ZDR can be required per model group; downstream providers keep their own separate retention terms, so OpenRouter is a router, not a retention guarantee, on its own [40][41].
- **Hugging Face Inference Providers** — HF's own routing layer does not store request/response bodies for training; logs capped ~30 days, deletion within 90; SOC2 Type 2 on the Hub; but HF explicitly warns the *selected downstream provider* has its own separate policy — same caveat as OpenRouter [42].
- **Mistral (La Plateforme)** — Mistral is the only major AI lab headquartered in the EU, natively under GDPR, EU-hosted by default; standard API retains input/output 30 rolling days for abuse monitoring unless Zero Data Retention is purchased on the Scale plan (stateless endpoints only); self-hosting the open weights is also offered for zero external exposure [43][44][45].
- **DeepSeek's own API** — Inputs processed and stored in China by default, used to train/improve models unless the user opts out; no published fixed retention window or ZDR tier; a January 2025 incident reportedly exposed a public DeepSeek database (chat logs, API keys); falls under China's National Intelligence Law reach [46][47]. **Poor goodwill** — avoid; use a third-party host for these weights instead.
- **Moonshot AI (Kimi's own API)** — Privacy policy reportedly allows training on prompts/uploads with no documented consumer opt-out as of mid-2026; storage jurisdiction left vague in the policy itself (said to be outside the user's home country), though the consumer entity of record is Singapore with China-based engineering/infra; same National Intelligence Law exposure as DeepSeek [48][49]. **Weakest goodwill of the group reviewed** — again, reachable more safely through a neutral third-party host.
- **Nebius AI Studio** — EU data processor under GDPR/CCPA; enterprise-grade encryption; SCCs and EU-US DPF for transfers; deletes data when no longer legally needed rather than holding it indefinitely [50][51].
- **Scaleway / OVHcloud** — Both French, EU-hosted, GDPR-compliant, marketed with zero data retention and no training on prompts for their AI inference endpoints; both appear on France's HDS certified-hoster register (checked by the source as of September 2026); serve open models including Mistral and GLM variants directly [52][53].

## 3. Ranked shortlist

**1. DeepSeek V3.2 (or the newer V4-Flash for cost) via Fireworks AI, with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B running locally on Prometheus.** This is the strongest match to "doubting, conceptual, epistemological": the R1 lineage is the one model family with a *publicly documented reputation* for visible self-correction and backtracking in its own chain of thought [9][10][11], and V3.2/V4's Speciale variant reportedly reaches gold-medal-level scores on IMO/IOI/ICPC [1][2] — genuinely hard, adversarial, "did-I-miss-something" reasoning territory. Fireworks gives default zero-retention plus a wide compliance stack (SOC2/HIPAA/ISO) [33][34][35], routing entirely around DeepSeek's own China-hosted, opt-out-by-default API and its 2025 exposure incident [46][47]. The real gap: the only local sibling is a cross-architecture distill onto Qwen/Llama, not a shrunk V3.2/V4 — so the "small sibling" running on Prometheus won't behave identically to the cloud flagship, only in the same reasoning style transferred by distillation.

**2. Qwen3.8 (or Qwen3.5-27B for a lighter cloud tier) via DeepInfra or Together, with a same-family dense Qwen sibling (e.g. Qwen3-32B or Qwen3.5-27B) on Prometheus.** This is the cleanest fit to "a smaller variant of the same model family... usable in the cloud for public work" and locally on Prometheus, because Qwen is the one family here that ships one continuous architecture lineage from sub-1B to trillion-plus scale [12][13][14], so the local sibling is a true miniature of the cloud model rather than a distillation onto someone else's base. License is the cleanest of the group (Apache 2.0 with no revenue gates on any size actually needed here) [12][13]. Its general-reasoning standing is solid but not top-of-class (40 on the Artificial Analysis Index, behind GLM-5.3/Kimi K3/DeepSeek V4 [21]), and I found no evidence it has a distinctive "doubting" reputation the way DeepSeek does — it reads as the safe, well-rounded, well-licensed choice rather than the most skeptical one.

**3. GLM-4.6 (or current GLM-5.3) via Fireworks/Together/OpenRouter, with GLM-4.5-Air (106B-A12B) as the nearest same-family sibling for Prometheus/cloud secondary use.** GLM currently sits at or near the top of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index among open-weight models [20][21], MIT licensed [17], and GLM-4.6 was reported near-parity with Claude Sonnet 4 on a real human-evaluated coding benchmark [17] — a decent proxy for critique-adjacent quality even without a dedicated skepticism eval. GLM-4.5-Air is a genuinely small (relative) sibling that fits 128GB comfortably [18], better local fit than Kimi (no small sibling at all) though the *naming discontinuity* between "4.5-Air" and the "5.x" flagship line means the sibling may already be a generation behind the cloud model by the time this is set up — worth confirming the current Air/Flash equivalent at build time.

**Considered and set aside:** Kimi K2/K3 — best agentic/coding numbers of the group [15][16][22] but no small sibling at any size fits 128GB, and Moonshot's own privacy posture is the weakest reviewed [48][49] (mitigated only by not using Moonshot's own API). gpt-oss-120b — cleanest US jurisdiction and license, cheapest and most widely hosted [26][27][54], but no independent doubt/critique evidence found, and my unverified impression is that its heavy safety-refusal tuning may look more like caution than genuine epistemic doubt. Llama 4 and Mistral Large 3 — solid, permissively licensed, but neither surfaced independent evidence of critique quality strong enough to beat the three above.

## What I could not verify

- No published benchmark directly measures "doubt"/refusal-to-bluff/critique quality *across* DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, and GLM head-to-head; the epistemic-humility work I found (BullshitBench, HumbleBench) evaluates closed frontier models like Claude, not this open-weight set [55][56].
- I did not fetch primary license text for DeepSeek V4-Pro/V4-Flash directly (only search-snippet mentions of a LICENSE file existing) — confirm the exact MIT wording before relying on it commercially [4].
- Current (September 2026) per-token pricing and rate limits for Fireworks/Together/DeepInfra on the specific models above came from secondary aggregator pages, not each provider's own live pricing page — re-check before committing spend [22][54].
- Whether GLM-4.5-Air/5.3-Flash or the Qwen small siblings have officially blessed GGUF builds (versus community quantizations) that are verified to run well on AMD Strix Halo specifically — not confirmed; only general "fits under X GB" claims were found [18][19].
- Scaleway/OVH/Nebius's GDPR and HDS claims were read from secondary summaries, not the providers' own DPAs in full text [50][51][52][53].
- Groq's and DeepInfra's SOC 2 status was read from their own docs/blog pages, not an independent certificate registrar [36][38][39].
