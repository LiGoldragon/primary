## Reversible local action

Before the one saved-profile reconnect, I changed only the existing `goldragon.criome` NM profile: `ipv4.never-default=yes`, `ipv6.never-default=yes`, `ipv4.ignore-auto-dns=yes`, and `ipv6.ignore-auto-dns=yes`. Their prestate was `no` for all four. This was the first configuration change in this outage incident; all earlier observations were read-only. It prevents a later successful management association from replacing Ouranos's wired default or DNS. After the timed-out activation, the wired default remained unchanged. I retained these safety settings for the existing autoconnect profile; exact rollback is `nmcli con mod goldragon.criome ipv4.never-default no ipv4.ignore-auto-dns no ipv6.never-default no ipv6.ignore-auto-dns no`. No connection, secret, or interface was deleted or recreated. Future automatic retries also use these settings. If the profile later associates, Wi-Fi will deliberately supply neither a default route nor DNS; a later reachability result must be interpreted with that change in mind. These settings protect Ouranos's primary connection; they did **not** repair the failed association.

The preceding uplink report concluded another manual reconnect would duplicate NM's automatic attempts. I made one explicit activation because the newly observed beacon made the outcome newly informative. That reversal was deliberate and bounded to one activation, with no further manual retry. It stopped in 802.11 authentication: the kernel reports three authentication transmissions without an answer, and there is no observed association, four-way handshake, DHCP or IP address. This locates the observed failure **before** DHCP, DNS and routing and provides no evidence of a wrong passphrase; it does not prove that the AP received the frames or identify range, radio, AP service, peer OS or hardware as the cause.
