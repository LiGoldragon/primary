## What each mark means, in every book

| mark | means |
|---|---|
| blue, thick outline | the one thing you rule on |
| green with + | added, works |
| red-orange with − | removed, broken, a risk |
| amber with ? | an open question |
| grey, thin | context, unchanged |
| purple, a person | you |
| dashed | proposed |
| solid | exists now |
| a box around things | they belong together |

Colour is never alone: each one carries a sign too, so the meaning survives for any eye.

## The rules of a good figure

- One figure says one thing.
- A figure is drawn only where the idea has a shape: a branch, a loop, a join, a comparison. A straight line of steps stays a sentence.
- Boxes hold a few words; the verb sits on the arrow.
- Nine boxes at most; a bigger idea becomes a sequence of figures.
- Amounts are drawn to scale.
- A change is drawn in one frame: what is added, what goes, what stays.
