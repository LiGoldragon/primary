# Messages that mean something

Every message the Core Secondary received from other flows between October 10 and October 16 was read and sorted by what it gave the reader.
More than four in ten were useless.

| Class | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Carries an action for the recipient | 155 |
| A blocker | 39 |
| A result the recipient needed to act | 180 |
| Useless | 274 |
| All received | 648 |

Most of the useless ones came from Field: 147 of the 211 sent by its Core Secondary, and 21 of the 26 sent by the committer.
Mind's Core sent 142, and 55 of them were useless.
Psyche's seats sent the fewest useless messages.

The four clearest:

1. A message whose whole body was a six-character commit hash.
2. A report that four requested files were published, followed by two machine flags.
3. A report that a stopped task had changed nothing, followed by a list of the nine kinds of change it had not made.
4. "Withdrawal received. My earlier whole answer had already transported before your withdrawal; I will send no further answer to that question."

The senders were doing what they were told.
The skills tell a flow to name the commit that proves its work, and to report the messenger's printed receipt.
The committer's brief tells it to reply with every commit.
The Core Secondary's own orders asked for a commit 53 times and for a lock-release reply 13 times.
The bare hash answered its order to send the commit as six characters.

The rules that told the senders to report this way are being changed now, without your ruling.
The three proposals below change how messages are meant to work.

## Proposal 1: psyche-context › skills/vision-messaging.md

- Send only messages that require the recipient's action, deliver a result it awaits, or report an error or blocker affecting its work; keep routine receipts in durable records for requested status reports.
+ A message means something to its recipient: an action it must take, a blocker in its way, or a result it needs in order to act.
+ Nothing else is sent: no receipt, acknowledgement, bare hash or identifier, status narration, restated order, evidence path, native session identifier, or list of what was not done.
+ Proof that work happened stays in the sender's own records; it is never sent, and never asked for.

Ruling: land, amend, or reject.

## Proposal 2: psyche-context › skills/vision-messaging.md

+ An order says what result its sender needs in order to act, or asks for nothing and then hears only of a blocker.
+ An order never asks for a commit, a receipt or a confirmation.
+ A hash or an identifier is sent only inside a sentence that tells the recipient what to do with it, never as a whole message.

Ruling: land, amend, or reject.

## Proposal 3: psyche-context › skills/vision-messaging.md

+ The messenger refuses a message whose body is only a hash, an identifier, a link without a sentence, or an acknowledgement, and tells its sender why.
+ A recipient that gets a useless message tells its sender once, in one line, which kind it was.
+ A refused or useless message becomes a lesson in the sender's own compensation, so that no flow sends it again.

Ruling: land, amend, or reject.
