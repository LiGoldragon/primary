## Admit the missing pieces before fitting the puzzle
"It's the same phenomenon I was talking about when I was relating it to a child trying to force the pieces of a jigsaw puzzle together because he just wants to fit the pieces together. He doesn't care about putting the image together correctly. That's something.

It's a smell I get a lot from AI: it doesn't try to figure out what parts of the puzzle it doesn't have. Instead it just fits all the pieces together no matter what: no matter if the border actually makes a line or the shape of the outside of the puzzle, or no matter if the colors line up or the pieces even fit together properly. It just pounds it with a hammer and jams them together. That's what that book reads like and it means that you guys need to ask me more questions.

Instead of making a proposal, if you don't have enough data, if you're missing pieces of the puzzle, we need to make a concept out of this that sits really high in the system prompt to guide the agents to start. It's like I said: first you have to admit what you don't know. First you have to make a list of unknowns when judged against a complete concept, which you could say is a complete being. A good idea is like a person: it holds together, it's consistent, and it has a certain total personality that seems congruent with the rest.

When you design a system you need to have, I guess, a role model or a golden system that you can compare against to see if you're currently in possession of enough data to put together all of the anatomy of the system and design."
Context: said of the book «The main flow delegates», which a check found had overreached the records.
-- psyche, STT, 2026-10-12.
