Presentation.{ «Meaning and vocabulary» }

How words are chosen and kept: one English vocabulary shared by every flow and by you, the meaning language that will one day replace loose prose with types, and the standing rulings on which words are used and which are never said.

### The meaning language

The meaning language is a written language of signs that stand for meanings, not sounds, built from typed structures and joined into one ontology.

> the meaning language ... is the specified language: the logical language, a little bit like Hanzi. ... purely logographic as a computer language that is specified with structs and enums

-- 19 September 2026

It is for every layer of the system, and it can be rendered into any human language, English first.

> all the layers of the system have this meaning language, which is both enums and structs and scale scalars that can be translated into natural language ... It's going to create the ontology of this meaning language, which would then translate to any language. We're creating the English user interface first because we're bootstrapping in English.

-- 24 September 2026

Its anatomy comes from Pāṇini's grammar of Sanskrit.

> It's going to be the base for everything: how our system thinks, communicates, and classifies things in the world.

-- recorded 8 September 2026

Its roots are the seven Vaiśeṣika categories.

> Well, it's pretty clear that we're going with Vaiśeṣika here

-- 19 September 2026

Everything becomes a type. A free-text name is only a type not yet finished, and plain text survives only as the last step, display.

> Ultimately everything becomes a type because everything is a variant of a set. ... There are these open-ended variants, which we call names.

-- 26 September 2026

> The string is an unspecified program. It's unfinished computer science. The final computer science only has a string in display data.

-- 26 September 2026

Meaning lives in datom, and it is never mixed with plain strings.

> there is no more meaninigOrString. strings are strings, and meaning is meaning

-- 3 September 2026

The verbs are Sanskrit and define the situation of a statement.

> We use Sanskrit. There are all these different situations, and that's what all these different verbs define: these different situations, the different relations of time, people, numbers, gender, and intention.

-- 19 September 2026

The structure comes first: one root of expressible kinds, held singly or as a list.

> There's a root variant, so you can have a vector of these or a single of these, right? You have these two main types, which you could also give a name to.

-- 19 September 2026

Meanings carry annotations, a few layers deep, and a changed meaning is a new meaning.

> If you add a layer, you're really adding a layer of annotation, commenting on this first layer of meaning, and then you can comment on the comment

-- 19 September 2026

> If a meaning is changed, its identity changes because now it could mean something quite different just because of a small alteration.

-- 19 September 2026

It grows in steps. The first version is a variant or two with text inside, and the text shrinks until none is left.

> The first version of sema could be that it just has one or two layers of variants ... and the payload at the end being a string. That way we get a sort of strongly typed string

-- 26 September 2026

> When SEMA is fully developed there are no more strings because we can express anything through the structure of the SEMA specification.

-- 26 September 2026

## 2. What exists today

The vocabulary skill defines 14 terms, counted in its source: flow, flow identity, flow directory, thread, transcript, living messenger, illustrated book, witness, quackery, the living, past, base context, vision impurity and machine. It also carries two rules: a defined term overrides competing words, and machine and flow replace AI and agent. The copies Claude and Codex flows load match the source.

Layer, voice, book, kind and the meaning language are not defined there. "Seat" still appears in 11 of the 70 authored skills; "voice" appears in 4.

The meaning language exists as a proposal library, marked not adopted. It declares 45 types and 92 variants, each with a Sanskrit and an English name, and its own check confirms every name has both. Nothing reads or writes it yet.
