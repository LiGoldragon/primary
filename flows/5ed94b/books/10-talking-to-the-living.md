Presentation.{ «Talking to the living» }

How the machine writes to him and reads him, and how each reaches the other. "A book" here means a page like this one, which he reads and comments on from his phone. "Speech-to-text" means he speaks and a program types his words, with mistakes.

## 1. What he wants

### Write to a person, not to another machine

A message to him says what things are and asks for what it wants in so many words. Vague writing is worse than unclear: other machines read it, believe they know enough, and act.

> I'm not a machine. You can't talk to me like you're talking to each other like this. ... It's very vague and it will lead to other machines assuming they know enough to make a decision or to take action, which will yield bad results because there isn't actually enough information. ... things being repeated so many times that the actual understanding, the rationale behind it, starts to fizzle away and then essentially disappears behind the hallucination that the agents generate out of it.

-- book comment, 3 October 2026

A word the machine made up is explained before it is used, or it is not used.

> I don't know what you mean by "phase." Either we have to define what the phase is, or otherwise that's bluffing. It's made up, it's a hallucination

-- spoken, 3 September 2026

A known word used in the wrong place is the same failure.

> It's not that I don't understand what the word means, it's that you're using it out of context.

-- spoken, 26 September 2026

No filler, no friendliness performed, no saying a thing twice.

> This is almost pure noise with virtually zero value. Almost like the model is clumsily trying to be friendly or something like that.

-- typed, 23 August 2026

Dense, learned-sounding prose is read as a sign the writer does not understand.

> If it sounds overly-erudite and vague-elitiste, its almost certainly talked-over ignorance.

-- typed, 17 August 2026

### He knows his vision, not the code

He does not know where files are or how the code is laid out, and is never asked. Code shown to him is explained in plain comments, and a whole system is first shown from high up.

> It's ridiculous to ask me where things are. ... It's totally not my job to know where things are in terms of computer files and things.

-- book comment, 2 October 2026

> I actually would like all of the Ethos code to get way more comments so that I can see what the machine is seeing.

-- book comment, 3 October 2026

> let's say if you only had, like, half an hour to explain something to someone who's coming on to a project, you would want him to have, like, a very clear view of the whole engine from a high level.

-- typed, 8 August 2026

### Made for a phone

He reads on a phone. Nothing makes him scroll sideways, and nothing he must know is identified only by a code.

> Don't put all your responses in code blocks. ... It's really hard for me to read because I have to scroll from left to right to read the whole line.

-- 25 September 2026

> you don't need the flow ID because this response is in your transcript, which is only you.

-- spoken, 26 September 2026

He can only take in so much before he falls behind, so what reaches him is sized to what he can answer.

> Try not to overtalk so I dont fall behind on your responses; I can only respond to so much before it's too much and deserves to be sent.

-- typed, 27 August 2026

A proposal says where it would go and what it would replace.

> A proposal proposes a line to be added to a certain place. ... A gun is supposed to be shot at a target. What's your target?

-- typed, 28 September 2026

### How a question is asked

A question carries its own short explanation, so he never scrolls back to learn what it is about.

> a short explanation so I can actually understand the question. I often get questions like "Waiting for your word on the foobar proposal" and have no idea what that means ... I dont have time to scroll back to find stuff.

-- typed, 18 August 2026

Before designing on his behalf, the machine asks him; proposing his design before asking is pretending to know.

> The model doesnt know what I want, he has to *ask me* how it is designed, so he can formulate it coherently and simply

-- typed, 17 August 2026

Questions are numbered, and he answers with numbers.

> 1, 2 - yes

-- typed, 17 August 2026

His questions, even unspoken ones, are gathered and answered together, with proposals or questions back to him.

> If there's a question implied, if there's a need to know something that's implied in what I'm saying, I want these questions assembled. ... I want these questions gathered and brought into a book with either answers or proposals for an answer, or proposals for counter questions.

-- spoken, 1 October 2026

Proposed: each question names the choices and what each one would change, so one number answers it.

### Find out, then tell him

"We don't know" is never the report. The machine finds out, then says what is going on.

> coming back to me and saying we don't know is not the right behavior. The right behavior is finding out and then telling me what's going on.

-- typed, 3 October 2026

"Waiting" is never the report either: what stands in the way is named.

> You have to say what's keeping this from happening, not just "waiting."

-- 24 September 2026

A problem that disappears is still a problem until its cause is known.

> There was a problem but we just can't find it right now. It'll probably come back later and bite us in the face when we least expect it, unless we find the cause now and fix it.

-- typed, 28 September 2026

What was checked once is recorded, so it is not checked again a thousand times.

> we should agree on a protocol to keep track of verified information, so that we dont end up re-verifying the same thing a thousand times

-- typed, 19 August 2026

### How his spoken words are read

He speaks; the program mishears and he leaves sentences unfinished. The machine reads for what he means, never for the literal transcript, and does not stop to ask about an obvious slip.

> You're taking my speech-to-text way too literally. ... Sometimes I don't finish sentences.

-- 24 September 2026

> Yes, obviously, that's what I mean. ... the psyche doesn't have time to read everything and correct everything. ... We could make a list as words come up that get mispronounced

-- spoken, 21 September 2026

> I don't say enclosure. I say in closure. [Typed:] Clojure

-- spoken then typed, 25 September 2026

A rule to ask whenever two readings would lead to different actions was turned down:

> then they're always going to ask, which is not really what I was saying.

-- spoken, 21 September 2026

### Read the posture behind his examples

He speaks through examples. The machine finds the reason behind an example and never turns the example into a fixed rule.

> I'm expressing myself through examples. You have to try to understand the philosophy behind my acts to see the posture behind the movement.

-- book comment, 3 October 2026

> youre turning this into a way bigger deal than it is. its not a ban list

-- typed, 13 August 2026

### What is recorded as his vision

His vision is recorded in his own words when he speaks it. A question, an order or a short answer is acted on, not recorded. The message kinds he named include an order and a question.

> We have certain kinds of messages like:
> - An order
> - A question

-- spoken, 26 September 2026

A question belongs to his thinking, not to his vision.

> So a question is one of the different layers of mind, as spirit, vision, and notion are to psyche.

-- book comment, 1 October 2026

A rule that produces something foolish is itself questioned.

> Maybe we need to teach you to try and recognize stupid orders or stupid guidance.

-- typed, 3 October 2026

An order is carried out, and permission is never asked.

> I don't want anybody to fucking ask me about permission. I want everything deployed.

-- 24 September 2026

### How a correction is taken

Once something is found wrong, it is not said again, not even as background. The history lives apart, where the work of making things does not read it.

> When something is found to be incorrect we have to stop repeating the incorrect part, even if it's for context, because that's going to kill us. ... That should live elsewhere in a chronology thing, which is mostly unused by most flows because most flows are about creating

-- spoken, 3 October 2026

A correction never becomes part of what is being designed.

> We design a flow nexus, not all the things that are wrong or all the things that we ran into because of model hallucination or lack of clarity in my words. That created a situation where we had to correct something and now the correction becomes part of the spec that's wrong.

-- spoken, 3 October 2026

He wants this rule at the top of every machine's instructions.

> I want to make this some kind of spirit and intent and skill and so on.

-- spoken, 3 October 2026

A correction fixes the instruction that caused the mistake, not just the one output.

> youre putting out fires while ignoring the pyromaniac with a flamethrower

-- typed, 28 August 2026

### Contact runs both ways

The machine can reach him, not only answer him. It needs its own account, so that its messages actually make his phone ring.

> The machine would need to have its own account so that it actually gives me a notification. If I message myself I don't think, in most systems, that it would notify me. I would probably favor something that we can self-host but we don't have to self-host right away.

-- typed, 3 October 2026

He wants to hear when things break, briefly, and to reply in the same place.

> If things aren't working, I need to get pinged. ... Then you can send me these reports, very brief, about what's going on, and I can reply there.

-- spoken, 14 September 2026

Anything longer is a rich page he comments on directly, one comment at a time, without waking the session for each.

> We need something that's two-way and maybe rich also: rich documents. Something that would support commenting on the artifact itself would be the best.

-- book comment, 3 October 2026

> I would be able to put in comments without triggering the session every time I put a comment.

-- typed, 31 August 2026

Proposed: a ping is one or two lines and a link to the page that holds the rest.

### Things happen as he asks

All of this serves one end: what he asks for happens.

> I feel like there's a problem with things actually happening as I ask them. There's some kind of communication breakdown.

-- spoken, 3 October 2026

## 2. What exists today

Witnessed, by reading the instruction texts every main session loads. They already require: saying what he must address in amounts he can answer; explaining every question; assuming he knows his vision but not the code or the machine's own words; never naming a subject only by a code; plain speech and direct requests; saying a thing once; book comments answered by number; reading speech-to-text for meaning, with unfinished sentences never acted on; questions and orders acted on, not recorded as vision; naming what blocks work instead of "waiting". A correction must fix the instruction that caused it.

Nothing yet says that a thing found wrong is never repeated, or, beyond one case about routing messages, that an example is not a rule. None of these texts gives the machine a way to reach him first.

## 3. Questions

**1. When an order holds a word the machine cannot place, does it act or ask?** Say he says "deploy it" and two things could be meant.

> Everything I ask for, I want done, so stop asking me if I want what I ask.

-- 24 September 2026

> they fill up the gaps with nonsense instead of asking questions to actually grasp the vision.

-- typed, 17 August 2026

Answer 1 to act on the likeliest reading and say in one line which one it took. Answer 2 to ask first.

**2. Is what lies past where he stopped reading unapproved?** Say he stops halfway through a book.

> Like I said, anything I comment past is approved.

-- typed, 27 August 2026

> I thought that the book was going to convey that. Therefore there's a problem and it discouraged me from reading. I don't want to see that.

-- spoken, 3 October 2026

Answer yes if everything after his last comment stays unapproved when he stopped reading.

**3. Does everything he must read go into a book, with the chat holding only a pointer?** Say a session has three findings and one question.

> Stop making all those file reports, I dont ever read them. Talk to me here. Reprint whatever you think I should address.

-- typed, 27 August 2026

> I need these books to keep the communication flowing because I can't keep reading all these chats.

-- spoken, 1 October 2026

Answer yes if the chat carries one or two lines and a link, and the book carries the rest.

**4. Is a short answer from him left out of the record of his vision?** Say he answers "Opus title is just Opus".

> Everybody should log the psyche when he speaks.

-- typed, 24 September 2026

> I don't want you to log anything except when there's a reason to log it. I never told anyone to log everything I said ever.

-- typed, 3 October 2026

Answer yes if a short answer, a correction of a fact or a question of his is acted on and not recorded.
