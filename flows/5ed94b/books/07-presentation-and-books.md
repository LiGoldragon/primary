# Presentation and books

How the machine talks to him: the living messenger, what one message in it holds, how he answers, how a drawing is made, and how the series about the vision is kept.

## Part 1. What he wants

### The living messenger is how the machine reaches him

The messenger is the user interface. Whatever a flow wants him to know goes into it; what stays in a chat is not read.

> Stop saying "page." First of all I don't say "page," I say "book" but it's not the right term either. It's the user interface. It's the living messenger. That's how you message me; it's how you talk to me. Everything that isn't going into that user interface is probably not going to be read by the living, which means it's useless if the AI is trying to communicate with me through its chat without that becoming a book.

-- 2 October 2026

> you know that I don't read your responses, right? I only read the presentations so why didn't you put that into a presentation? ... Talk back to me in the book.

-- 2 October 2026

It exists because the chat scrolls past him, and he reads it on his phone.

> My whole point is that, with the chat's vertical scrolling, I miss everything. The thing I need is not in the final answer.

-- 28 September 2026

> I just remote-access the sessions from my phone. The only thing I really look at is a little bit of the chats when I'm on my phone and the UI presentations that we're doing through Claude.

-- 2 October 2026

Turning a presentation into a message is mechanical work. In time a program and an app of his own do it; Claude's commentable pages stand in until then (proposed).

> My point, my whole point, is that the books become the user interface. ... really rendering is mechanical when you think about it. We should be able to just write a tool that does it.

-- 1 October 2026

What one message is called is still open: see question 1.

### One message is one presentation

A main flow's presentation is its real work, and each presentation becomes a new message.

> The presentation is what they're there for. That's their main output and then we can turn that into books or a new version of a book and we always just make a new one.

-- 30 September 2026

The flow marks where the presentation starts and ends in its conversation. The marks are for the machine and need not show.

> We're going to train all of the main flows to, whenever they want to talk to the living, mark that block as intended for the living at the beginning and at the end.

-- 1 October 2026

> I don't need to be able to see them in the chat. I think if we keep everything else that the machine says to a more mechanical logging-like behavior, then it'll be obvious when it's actually talking in [prose] and talking to me.

-- 1 October 2026

Its first line is one short line naming the title, as this book's first line does, and nothing else, not even the sender.

> Yeah the metadata is one datom line. That's brilliant. I love it. Let's do it.

-- 1 October 2026

### What a message holds

A plain title.

> I like better titles, no convoluted titles like that.

-- 3 October 2026

Only a few points, simply put.

> Create a more direct, visual, and simple presentation of the most central simple concepts that we need to investigate and resolve, presented very simply and with only a few of them. I don't want to be showered with too much data.

-- 30 September 2026

More drawing than paragraph.

> I want a lot of visuals actually. I'm kind of lacking visuals. My eyes get tired by long paragraphs and I'd rather just see what you're trying to show me with a flowchart that has been properly rendered.

-- 2 October 2026

Prose written for a person, not for another machine.

> I'm not a machine. You can't talk to me like you're talking to each other like this.

-- book comment, 3 October 2026

The design as the flow sees it, its questions, what it judged best, and the alternatives.

> Psyche showing the design, the questions, the things that we did and judged best, and what other alternatives there are, maybe

-- 29 September 2026

Each point and each choice carries a number he can name in a comment (proposed; it is how he already answers).

> Skill catch-up wave, approved: 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 13, 14

-- 2 October 2026

### What a message never has

No swiping sideways. It is one column that scrolls down.

> I don't want this swipe-left-to-right type of web UI anymore. The cloud interface for it is horrible because it captures the movements. No, I always scroll up and down

-- book comment, 2 October 2026

No buttons, notes, checkboxes or other controls. Only comments reach the machine.

> use the pages without the buttons because I even tried to take a note and save it and I can't see it. I think the only thing that works really is the comment so we might as well just get rid of the buttons and all that and I'll just comment

-- 28 September 2026

> I checked the boxes in the UI. There were checkbox checks so let's see if you see them. If not we'll have to prohibit their use.

-- book comment, 2 October 2026

No prose in code boxes.

> This code block is probably appropriate maybe if you're writing code but this makes your prose really hard to read.

-- 28 September 2026

No Sonnet notes in the margin.

> I don't want these Sonnet comments anymore. Sonnet's place is not in comments so that was an error on my part.

-- book comment, 2 October 2026

Nothing that needs zooming on a phone held upright. Code, when there is code, runs downward and lined up.

> We're going to be on a phone, so everything has to be huge and vertical. ... phones cannot be used sideways.

-- 20 September 2026

> I want it to be a language that expands vertically ... perfectly lined up, beautifully formatted, a bit like Nix or Python.

-- 2 October 2026

### How he answers

He answers by commenting on the message itself. A comment belongs to the exact point it sits on, which is one more reason sideways pages are forbidden.

> I was commenting on item 2 drawn and again as you can see, the swiping left/right thing is breaking where the comments are. ... We need to absolutely forbid this kind of UI.

-- book comment, 2 October 2026

A message he has commented on is never edited and sent again. The reply is a new message.

> And we can't reuse a book that I've commented on because then the comments are still there even if you edit the book. If something's been changed the comment doesn't apply properly anymore so they're like throwaways.

-- 2 October 2026

The flow receives a comment with just what it answers: his words and the place in the source.

> It could just relate it back to the markdown: where in the markdown is the part that I commented on?

-- 16 September 2026

A comment is an example of a way of thinking, not a new law.

> I'm expressing myself through examples. You have to try to understand the philosophy behind my acts to see the posture behind the movement.

-- book comment, 3 October 2026

What he has not answered stays alive: it is carried into the next message, and flows remind him of it.

> I can't read them all so it has to end up in the flashbook and I have to be reminded of it by different flows. Have you read this flashbook? Just keep reminding me so I can comment on it.

-- 24 September 2026

### How a drawing is made

Drawings are flowcharts written by hand in SVG for the phone held upright: readable without zoom, richer than black boxes and arrows, never Mermaid.

> I want the flowcharts properly done in SVG so that they render well on a small screen in mobile vertical portrait mode. I want to be able to read the charts without zooming in and I want the flowcharts to be visually enhanced more than just black and white arrows and boxes.

-- 2 October 2026

> The latest books were rendered with this stupid fucking mermaid chart rendering that we can't fucking read for shit, and we weren't supposed to do these anymore.

-- 3 October 2026

The drawing itself is expressive: an illustrated flowchart.

> This is an example of a good or decent illustration which could be made from a flowchart accompanied by prose. The sonnet model can take the flowchart and give it more expressive power through this stylization. You could call that an illustrated flowchart.

-- book comment, 1 October 2026

> This is a great example of the visual flowcharts I want to see. Let's make sure that the skill has distilled guidance that is likely to result in this type of clear and visually enriched flowchart.

-- book comment, 2 October 2026

Every drawing carries information; prettiness alone is not enough.

> On the illustrations I don't need illustrations that don't convey anything. ... Our illustrations are supposed to convey information.

-- 24 September 2026

Fit is checked by measuring. No screenshots, and no images kept in the repository.

> Computers have enough data to know how to render things without needing screenshots, right?

-- book comment, 3 October 2026

> I don't want images committed in the workspace repo at all. ... I don't want any screenshots taken of the book. That's a waste of money

-- book comment, 1 October 2026

Whether there are generated pictures besides the flowcharts is open: see question 2.

### How the vision series is kept

The vision is written out in full as a series of books, one per subject. He reviews it; it is corrected and implemented, round after round.

> I want it all fucking fleshed out so I can review it, and we're just going to correct it and correct it and implement and correct and implement and correct. We're going to maintain a series of books about what we want, the vision.

-- 3 October 2026

What he approves becomes skills.

> And that vision becomes skills. It's the same thing, right? The data ends up in the skill.

-- 3 October 2026

Each round is a fresh set of books (proposed, following the rule against reusing commented books). An index keeps every round, so a subject's history can be followed.

> Let's keep an index of all these artifacts in there. When one of them gets updated, we can see maybe the history.

-- 16 September 2026

How the books are made is tried, and he rates the trials.

> Let's do a few trials with the books and how you can prompt the subagent to do it and then I'll rate what I see

-- 2 October 2026

Who makes a message is open: see question 3. Whether a new round shows what changed is open: see question 4.

## Part 2. What exists today

Witnessed by reading, not by running.

A book worker exists. Its instructions still describe one standing page kept up to date in place, with an answer button under each question: the opposite of comments only and a new message each time. It is set to run on Sonnet, though its text calls itself Opus. A small tested program cuts a flow's conversation into readable pieces for it.

The book skill says: one new presentation per message, never an update of a commented one; answers by comment; no controls; hand-written SVG drawings readable on a phone, never Mermaid. The flashbook skill adds vertical scrolling, no swiping, no checkboxes, at most four points, numbered choices, no screenshots, no committed images, yet still calls proposals "checkable". Both skills' written sources are newer than the copies flows load today; the phone-fit check exists only in the sources.
