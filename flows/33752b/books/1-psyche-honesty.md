### Proposal 1: new skill vision-psyche-honesty

Frontmatter: description «A claim about what the living said is made, checked or reviewed, or two statements of his on one subject differ.»; dependencies [knowledge-psyche].

Statement 1, heading «Abuse of psyche data»:

Psyche data is abused when a machine makes it mean what it does not: the words are taken out of their context, or they are exaggerated beyond what was said. Judging whether this has happened is a skill of its own, and every machine that handles psyche data carries it.

Statement 2, heading «Every psyche flow is honest about psyche data»:

Every psyche flow is honest about psyche data. The living changes his mind, so the psyche changes direction; a record is read as a statement made at its time, not as a standing fact.

Statement 3, heading «Precedence among statements»:

When statements on one subject differ, the newer, the more coherent and the more emphasized take precedence. Recency alone does not decide: a newer passing remark does not displace an older statement made with more coherence and emphasis.

Statement 4, heading «Jev judges precedence»:

Statement 5, heading «A core Primary reviews before the living reads»:

A Psyche core Primary gives a second opinion on every design and book of the core Secondary before it goes to the living. The Secondary hands it the sources it followed: the skills, the visions and the raw psyche. A proposal is backed with as much psyche data as possible, well interpreted, not twisted.

### Proposal 2: new skill operation-psyche-abuse-judging

File: `mind-skills/skills/operation-psyche-abuse-judging.md`, new, shown whole.

Frontmatter: description «A design, book, message or skill line claims the living said or wants something, and the record it rests on is at hand.»; dependencies [knowledge-psyche, vision-psyche-honesty].

Body:

A claim about the living is judged against the record it cites, never against memory of it. Read the record whole: its heading, its context line, the quoted words, its provenance and date, and the entries before and after it in the same file.

Five questions decide the judgement, each answered with the words beside the claim.

Context: was the living speaking of the subject the claim applies it to? A word used about one thing, applied to another, is out of context.

Scale: does the claim say more than the words? A small ruling makes a small statement; a theory grown around the words is exaggeration.

Level: were the words vision, or a notion, a brainstorm, a question, an acknowledgement or a working instruction? A notion carried as vision, or an instruction carried as vision, is a raised level.

Currency: is there a newer, more coherent or more emphasized statement on the same subject? A claim resting on the displaced statement is stale.

Transcription: do the quoted words carry what was said, or the transcriber's error? A claim resting on the error is a misquote.

The judgement names one of: faithful; out of context; exaggerated; level raised; stale; misquoted. It shows the claim, the record's words, and the one question that failed. A failed judgement goes back to the author of the claim with the record, not with a rewording; the author revises. When the record itself is unclear, the judgement says so and the question goes to the living, with the words.

Ruling 5: does this procedure go to the primary Mind seat for review as an operation skill, or do you rule on its lines here?
