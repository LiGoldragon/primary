+ Communication with the living carries the most to him for each moment of his attention. The same lines hold in every harness: Claude artifacts, Codex, or a later one.

+ It is multimodal: figure, text and speech. It is visual first, and its text is minimal.

+ It reaches him through the living messenger: one presentation per message, each a fresh item, holding whole everything he is to read or rule on.

+ It is read on a phone, scrolling top to bottom: one thing per section, each section and figure under its own heading, short text, much drawing.

+ A proposal is the least instruction that gives the biggest effect. It carries his words crystallized and names the records it draws from.

+ A proposal shows each change once, across every edition: the lines removed in red and the lines added in green, in a named module. A record to destroy or archive shows in its own colour, marked Destroy or Archive.

+ Code shown to him keeps every line within 52 characters.

+ A figure shown to him is a figure proposed for a module.

+ A figure is a flow answering one question in seconds: a box is a thing or a collection; an arrow is a one-way relation carrying its verb, so that box, verb, box reads as a sentence; nearness and enclosure are kinship; top to bottom is order; colour carries a role. A sizable presentation carries several figures.

+ A figure starts as a text sketch at most 52 characters wide. The drawn figure keeps every box, label and arrow of the sketch and adds style: its arrows and things may be illustrated with the feeling their meaning asks.

+ A choice is lettered alternatives, each a full line. He answers by comment and names a choice by its label, the letter or number; his comment is answered by the proposal it results in.

+ When he reviews proposal by proposal, each presentation carries one proposal.

+ A presentation of questions carries proposed answers and counterquestions.

### Proposal 2: vision-book keeps what is a book's own

File: `psyche-context/skills/vision-book.md`

Removed:

- dependencies: [vision-crystallization]

- A book is a proposal into context modules, and the whole of it is its crystallization: a line removed shows in red, a line added in green, and a figure in it is a figure proposed for a module.

- A figure shows how a thing moves, one question per figure, readable in seconds. Its grammar: a box is a thing or a collection; an arrow is a one-way relation carrying a verb, so that box, verb, box reads as a sentence; nearness and enclosure are kinship; top to bottom is order; colour carries a role or is absent. A list in boxes, a tally, or a sentence in a rectangle is not a figure. A diagram in a book's source is ASCII in that grammar, laid out vertically and no wider than 52 characters so a phone shows it; a sketch without arrows is rewritten before it is sent. The published book never shows the ASCII, but a figure drawn from it by a Tertiary or Quaternary subflow that is given the grammar with the sketch, keeps every box, label and arrow, and adds nothing the source does not say; a figure that lost an arrow is returned. A sizable book carries several figures.
