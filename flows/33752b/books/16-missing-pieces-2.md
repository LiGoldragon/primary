You asked for the concept of an ideal system and its anatomy, as the benchmark against which to ask what we are missing. Twelve thinkers were found; the figure sorts them. The anatomies they name, in their own words:

```
 THE ANATOMIES
 ┌──────────────────────────────┐
 │ Aristotle: material, form,   │
 │  source of change, that for  │
 │  the sake of which           │
 │ Vitruvius: durability,       │
 │  convenience, beauty         │
 │ Altshuller: engine,          │
 │  transmission, working unit, │
 │  control                     │
 │ Simon: goal, artifact,       │
 │  environment; the artifact   │
 │  is the interface            │
 │ Beer: five subsystems; a     │
 │  missing one is diagnosed    │
 │ Checkland: customers,        │
 │  actors, transformation,     │
 │  worldview, owners,          │
 │  environment                 │
 │ Alexander: fifteen           │
 │  properties of wholeness;    │
 │  good fit = no named misfit  │
 └──────────────────────────────┘
```

Three of them give a way to ask what is missing. Checkland walks each part of the ideal model and asks of the real thing: does it exist, how does it behave, how is it judged. Beer diagnoses by the absent subsystem. Alexander, in 1964, defines the ideal negatively: fit is the absence of every named misfit, and misfits are easy to name where fit is not. Ackoff alone orders the whole process ideal-first, and names the gaps as its output. No one found combines these; Rittel's objection, that what one needs to know depends on one's idea of the solution, is met by your wave: at every stage the question is asked again.

## Unknowns are hunted at every stage

Looking for unknowns is demonstrating doubt. It recurs like a wave: at every stage the question is asked again, what is it we don't know.

## Design blocks; prototype building documents

There are two modes. In design, an unanswered question blocks. In prototype building, the assumption taken is documented and the work goes on.

## No one knows ahead that the pieces are enough

No one can know ahead of time that the pieces are enough to yield the best version that could be come up with at the moment. Everybody contributes to the judgment, without limit. Judging that an idea is complete is the hardest part of a thinking machine to get.
