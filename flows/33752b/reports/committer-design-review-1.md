Review of «One Primary committer» by Fable 33752b: returned, not approved. Judged against the living's words of 2026-10-12 (one committer, a Field Quaternary on Luna, a turn-end hook only when Primary is dirty, FLOW_ID in the environment, in place and never a clone, jj squash, "I don't mind rewriting history", "just a simple system") and the spirit rule to start with the smallest shape that works.

What matches and stands: the seat (Field Quaternary, Luna, low); the hook fires only on a dirty Primary and a clean turn wakes nothing; the committer reads the firing session's transcript and writes the message from it; everything happens in the existing repository, no clone or worktree; tests on synthetic repositories; the committer never wakes itself.

Concrete changes required:

1. History rewrite. The living said late changes that belong to an earlier commit are squashed into it with jj and that rewriting history is fine, since we are the only writers. The design forbids rewriting published main and turns every late change into a successor commit. Replace: the committer squashes a late capture into the earlier commit it belongs to and moves main to the result; being the single publisher, its push of a rewritten main is safe. Keep non-force only as the default for a tip nobody rewrote.

2. The lock. The living said the committer holds the lock and everybody messages it. The design acquires PrimaryPublish per request and keeps Field's existing publisher as a second serialized publisher. Replace: the committer is the only thing that commits or pushes Primary and holds PrimaryPublish for its lifetime; no other publisher remains; the lock is not acquired and released per commit.

3. FLOW_ID. The living said the harness sets FLOW_ID in its environment and passes it to every subprocess so the hook can use it, instead of process inspection. The design treats FLOW_ID as insufficient and adds an enrollment binding flow, session, permitted paths and hook configuration. Replace: FLOW_ID from the environment identifies the owner; a flow owns its lane under flows/<FLOW_ID>/ and the paths its transcript shows it edited; no enrollment, no roster, no refusal on missing enrollment. A hook with no FLOW_ID reports that one fact.

4. Capture machinery. The design copies bytes into a receipt area with content hashes, detects busy files, keeps a private index, journals a compare-and-swap advance of shared HEAD, and preserves foreign staged entries. The living's shape: the hook messages the committer, the committer reads the transcript and commits. Replace: the committer commits, in place with jj, the owner's paths as they are when it runs; a file still changing is caught by that writer's next turn-end hook; two writers on one path are reported, not reconciled by snapshot machinery. Remove the snapshot, hash, journal and private-index layer; add any one piece of it back only when a test on the synthetic repository shows the simple shape loses work.

5. Size. With those four removed, the design and the proof list shrink to: hook → message → read transcript → jj commit of owner's paths → squash if it belongs to an earlier commit → push. Send the revised design whole; it is approved when it reads at that size and still holds the six points.
