# Bad on top of bad

Written by Fable. An account, not a proposal: nothing here is to land.

## What happened

The translator design was asked for in the chat. I wrote it as a book and launched the publisher. While the page rendered, the harness put a line in my prompt: say in a few words what you are doing, then continue. I answered it with the whole design, in the chat, and then gave the link. The living asked which of the two he was to read, and why there were two.

I named the cause as impatience and, asked to fix it, proposed a new line for intent:living-interaction: while a presentation is being made, the chat says so and nothing of what it holds; the harness line is answered with that one sentence.

He refused the line, and asked instead that what made me impatient be removed.

## What the refusal caught

The chain was three instructions deep.

```
harness line: say what you are doing
   |  produced
   v
whole design in the chat        <- the fault
   |  I answered with
   v
new belief: say one sentence     <- bad on bad
```

The harness line produced a behavior he does not want, which makes it a bad instruction. My proposed line contradicted it, which makes it a bad instruction too: two lines now pull against each other and the system has to hold both. Our modules already carried enough: the chat carries the title and link, a proposal with code goes to the presentation. The fault was not a missing belief; it was a foreign line that outweighed a belief we had.

In his words:

> If I hadn't asked the right question and just accepted your belief addition, then we would have piled bad instructions on top of bad instruction. An instruction that contradicts a previous instruction is bad because now it creates confusion in the system. An instruction that creates a behavior that we don't want to see is a bad instruction, which then qualifies that first midturn-injected instruction. They were both bad.

> You're adding bad on top of the already bad that's already there. If I hadn't asked the right question, the whole thing would have gone unnoticed and this probably happens a lot.

## Why it happens a lot

A correction arrives as a wish for different behavior. The nearest move is a line that asks for that behavior. A line is cheap to write, looks like care, and lands as belief. Nobody asks where the fault came from, so the instruction that produced it stays, and the new line is laid over it. Every such pair is a knot: two lines that must be read together to know what the flow is to do, and each one a place where the next harness line or the next correction trips. The modules grow, and each growth is a little less legible.

The right question was: which one, and why two. It asks for the cause. Once the cause is an instruction, the fix is its removal, and the belief that was already there does the rest.

## What holds

The lines that cover this already stand. Intent: a fault in conduct is a fault in belief. Vision: a line a module contradicts is edited by removing the contradicted line first; removal comes before addition. The case shows them at work against a line that is not ours: the harness's own. That line cannot be edited from a module. Its removal is being looked for in the harness now, and in the long term it is what the Flow Nexus's basic modules replace.
