## Contradictions between observers

1. **Prometheus's WAN address.** 753e69's "point-in-time peer evidence" has Prometheus `eno1` at `192.168.1.16/24` with default via `192.168.1.1` — Prometheus directly on the LAN. Every later observation has `eno1` at `10.44.0.148/24` via `10.44.0.1`. 753e69 flags its own figure as historical, so this is a stale-snapshot conflict rather than a live disagreement, but the two cannot both be current.
2. **Is Mega a different network from the LAN?** The living's words to 0ad137 say the Mega router "is a different network than the LAN, actually … There are two routers." 0ad137's own measurement contradicts this: associating to `Mega_2.4G_1896` produced `192.168.1.9/24` on the same `192.168.1.0/24` as the wired LAN, with no gateway offered and 100% loss to `192.168.1.1`. The published 0ad137 report records the address but does not state the contradiction.
3. **Timestamp frames.** 6db4fe and 753e69 treat Ouranos "local" as UTC−06:00. Ouranos's Yggdrasil journal lines read `2026/09/24 00:16:13` while the wall clock was 22:42 UTC on 09-23 — i.e. UTC+02:00. The UTC+02:00 reading is corroborated by Prometheus's independent clock (its `16:16:33` UTC−06:00 = 22:16:33 UTC, and the 25m58s peer uptime at 22:42:33 UTC). 0ad137's report acknowledges "mixed raw log timestamps" but resolves nothing. Any onset bracket built from Ouranos journal lines read as UTC−06:00 is off by eight hours.
4. **"Prometheus unreachable" vs. "getting internet from its Wi-Fi."** 6db4fe (09-22) concluded Prometheus was "not reachable through any witnessed route"; the living reported concurrently that their device had Internet through Prometheus's AP. Both can be true — the AP-side and the management-overlay side are independent — but no observer has ever witnessed both halves in one pass.
5. **Not a contradiction, worth separating:** 6db4fe's 09-22 11:27 finding treats the configured-name TCP/22 timeout as "Prometheus not reachable." 0ad137's 09-23 finding shows the same timeout coexisting with an instant TCP/22 connect on `10.44.0.148`. ## Observations the topology question still needs

**USB IPv6 state on both nodes** — Ouranos is settled: `ipv6.method=disabled`, `disable_ipv6=1`, no address, no listener. Prometheus's `eno1` side has a link-local and a listener. What is missing is Prometheus's **`accept_ra` / `disable_ipv6` sysctls on `eno1`** and whether anything on Prometheus filters IPv6 or multicast inbound on that interface — otherwise a repair that enables IPv6 on Ouranos's USB may still find the far side silent.

**Yggdrasil listen and peer configuration on both** — the *runtime* listeners are now known on both nodes. What is missing is the **configuration**: neither node's `yggdrasil.conf` `Listen`, `Peers`, `InterfacePeers`, `MulticastInterfaces` or `AllowedPublicKeys` has been read by anyone. On Ouranos this is blocked at the admin socket and at `sudo`; a privileged read (or a file read of the config) is the single highest-value missing observation. Without it, nobody can say whether Ouranos's absence of a USB listener follows from the disabled IPv6 or from a `MulticastInterfaces` regex that excludes `enp0s20f0u1c2`.

**Firewall rules per interface on both** — never successfully read live on either node. On Ouranos, `nft` was unavailable (09-21), `iptables-save` was permission-denied (09-21 audit and 09-22 round 2); the four `field-6db4fe-*` rules are known only from a transcript, not from a live rule dump, and their current presence after any reboot is unverified. Specifically needed: whether Prometheus's input policy on `eno1` admits UDP 10001 / TCP 10001 and IPv6 multicast, and what blocks TCP/80 on `10.44.0.148` while `*:80` listens. The living's own hypothesis ("maybe Yggdrasil is firewalled on that USB Ethernet device") is exactly this gap and remains untested.

**Multicast peering** — no observer has ever looked. No `ss -uln` for the Yggdrasil multicast port, no `MulticastInterfaces` config, no packet witness of link-local multicast on the USB segment. This is a complete blind spot and it is the mechanism the whole proposed repair depends on.

**Whether Prometheus's Wi-Fi AP clients reach Ouranos** — the only direct evidence is Ouranos itself holding `10.18.0.102/24` with a route via `10.18.0.1` on 09-21, before the outage. Zeus at `10.18.0.108`/`10.18.0.103` was shown reaching the *Internet* through `10.18.0.1`, which implies transit through Ouranos's NAT but was never traced to `10.44.0.1`. What is missing: from a current AP client, a ping or traceroute to **`10.44.0.1`** specifically, and Ouranos's NAT counters moving in response. The living's report that they have Internet from Prometheus's Wi-Fi is the strongest current evidence and it is a relayed claim, not a witness.
