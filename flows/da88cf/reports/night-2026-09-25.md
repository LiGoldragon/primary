**Cause, in plain words (seen):**

- Unknown: how it once worked before June. A saved profile ("li", node 2) shows that a login succeeded at some point.

**Decision taken, under your "I leave it to Fable's best judgment":**

- **Trust.** A cluster certificate authority (*CA*: one cluster-owned key that signs certificates) is declared in cluster data and installed on every machine. It is private, needs no public DNS, and works over Yggdrasil. The alternative was a public Let's Encrypt certificate, which would publish a name and need a Cloudflare token. A narrower option, trusting only the one Headscale certificate, was also weighed. The CA was kept because you said "the criome cluster is the trusted network".
- **Controller.** The controller stays on ouranos for now. Moving it to Prometheus is your call (question 7).
- **Joining.** Each machine joins by a small declared start-up step. It reads a one-time join key (a *preauth key*) from an encrypted secret file, without ever putting the key on a command line. The login address comes from cluster data, and the step runs only when the machine needs to log in. There is one reusable key per host.
- **Membership.** Membership is unchanged: ouranos and Prometheus, plus the declared test VMs.
- **Reset.** ouranos's hand-set login may be force-reset. Nothing depends on it.
- **Secrets.** A Field agent (b7da5d, "Field Sol") mints the secrets on ouranos as root and pipes them straight into the encrypted file, so no agent ever sees them. Seen: root on ouranos is reachable by key without a password. da88cf read your "get it fixed … best judgment" as the permission that the earlier plan said it needed.
- **Why:** it is the smallest change that follows the cluster topology and roles you asked for, keeps the network private, and puts every fact in cluster data.
