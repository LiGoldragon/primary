## 1. Quota is spent to a rhythm

- Lands in: `Vision/quotas.md` (new topic)
- Words replaced: 696. Statement: 194 words.

**Statement.** Quota is spent to a rhythm. Claude and Codex are each used at about a seventh of the week's allowance a day, so the two meet together at the end of the week; the five-hour window is the gauge of fair use for each, and an overused Fable falls back to Opus. When the hourly or daily share goes unused, light work runs to keep concepts materialized and testable, since unused quota is lost, and a reset close at hand may be used up harder. When one provider runs high, work on it waits at design while the other carries more: Opus main-flow jobs drive proofs of concept when Codex is idle, Sonnet takes small jobs, Haiku or Sonnet trivial ones. Priority goes to the core and the primary layer, which agree ideas and proofs of concept before the secondary implements them at scale. A proof of concept now is preferred to escalation; a later version changes what is disliked. What flows spend talking to one another is watched, and books are redone by cheap models. The slack is counted for the system to decide when to start flows, not for him to read.

## 3. The main flow's presentation becomes a new book

- Lands in: `Vision/livingMessenger.md`
- Words replaced: 603. Statement: 153 words.

**Statement.** A primary-layer flow's most important work is its view of a thing, and its presentation of that view is its main output. Whenever a main flow speaks to the living it marks that block as meant for him at its beginning and at its end, in an agreed pattern a tool can detect. The marks need not render: everything else the machine writes stays mechanical and log-like, so prose addressed to him stands out. Keeping the presentation simple is the main flow's own job. It passes the marked block down a layer, to a Sonnet subagent, which makes a book of it, always a new book, because a book he has commented on keeps its comments when edited and they no longer apply. That model knows the psyche: it checks the presentation against it and puts small, specially colored notes where it strongly agrees or disagrees, naming the psyche record and its age.

## 5. Judgment is sized to its call; judging and doing are split

- Lands in: `Vision/judgment.md` (new topic)
- Words replaced: 475. Statement: 170 words.

**Statement.** Where a thinking machine is needed, it is needed for judgment, and the judgment is sized to the call. Some work wants almost none: creating a session takes at most a tiny, fast, nearly free judgment call, like the judge that decides whether data fits the database. Where a result is plainly typed, as when a flow's last response is clearly a datom final response, reaping it needs little judgment; that flow checks for a replacement and, if none, tells the field to consider a continuation. Where much judgment is needed, as in auditing work against the vision by scanning the latest raw psyche and giving recency more power, a strong model does it in a subflow. Judging and doing are split: a careful model judges, for instance whether a flow is dead and has a successor, by reading its transcript, and its yes authorizes a cheap model to do the now trivial act. Where code cannot tell what is what, as in repairing a record, a thinking machine decides.
