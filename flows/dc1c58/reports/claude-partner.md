## Findings in one paragraph

Armando Torres (Armando Torres Aguilar, Mexico) has two books of
conversations with Carlos Castaneda. The first, *Encuentros con el
nagual* (2002, Spanish; English 2004 as *Encounters with the Nagual*),
contains the material closest to the requested topics: a chapter titled
"El camino del guerrero" / "The Path of the Warrior", explicit passages
on voluntad and intento, and two occurrences of "la meta". The four
phrases "La mitad del brujo", "La mitad del guerrero", "La mitad del
vidente" and "La mitad de la libertad" do not occur in either edition of
the first book, and Google Books finds no match for "la mitad del" in any
Torres title. Either they belong to the second book, whose text I could
not search, or they are the Book of Soul's own titles rather than
Torres's. That is an unknown, not a negative result.

## Structure of Encuentros con el nagual

Four parts; the chapters are grouped by theme, not by date.

- Parte 1, "Un romance con el conocimiento": La revolución de los brujos;
  La importancia personal; El camino del guerrero; Conciencia de la
  muerte; El drenaje energético; La Recapitulación; El umbral del
  silencio.
- Parte 2, "Diálogo de guerreros": eleven sections, about forty short
  chapters, including Saturación conceptual, Un inventario de creencias,
  Creer sin creer, La práctica del silencio, La oportunidad mínima, No
  se necesitan maestros, Conocerse a sí mismo, plants of power, stalking,
  dreaming, the assemblage point, the petty tyrant, the end of the
  lineage, the seers of the new era, the task of the nagual.
- Parte 3, "La regla del nagual de tres puntas": the thirty-page
  manuscript Torres says Castaneda charged him to publish, on renewing
  the lineages of knowledge. Chapters include El propósito de la regla
  and La tarea de los videntes actuales.
- Parte 4, "El mundo de los antiguos videntes hoy": Torres's own account
  of a group of sorcery practitioners.

## Where the requested topics live in the first book

Keyword counts, Spanish edition: guerrero 132, poder 72, brujo 56,
intento 47, libertad 31, propósito 21, voluntad 18, vidente 6, meta 3.

**El guerrero.** The chapter "El camino del guerrero" (Parte 1) is the
primary source. Its argument, summarized: every human is by right a
warrior who won a life-or-death race at conception; socialization
domesticates that inheritance; the warrior's war is against their own
weaknesses, not other people; the warrior's final challenge is to
balance all the attributes of the path, after which their purpose
becomes inflexible and they become owner of their will. The opening
chapter "La revolución de los brujos" carries the line that a warrior is
made to fight and that war is their rest.

**Voluntad (willpower).** Key statements, all in Parte 1 and early
Parte 2:

- Discipline governs attention and leads to will; will lets the warrior
  reshape the world as they want it rather than as imposed. For warriors,
  will is the antechamber of intent ("la voluntad es la antesala del
  intento").
- The tragedy of modern people is not their social condition but the
  lack of will to change themselves.
- Silence begins with an act of will that becomes the Eagle's command,
  but will must be transformed into intent; imposed silence is never
  real silence.
- Seeing reveals a supreme, impersonal will that organizes everything;
  sorcerers tune to it through inner silence.

**Poder (power).** Torres uses "poder" mostly in Castaneda's technical
senses: the nagual makes gifts of power; sorcerers put the power of
passion at the service of being, which drives the only search that
matters, the search for oneself; an apprentice who uses new power to
get rich is the example of a warrior who has not become owner of will.
Personal power as energy reserve is treated in "El drenaje energético"
and "La Recapitulación".

**La meta (the goal).** Two explicit statements:

- In the stalking chapter: breaking routines is only a means; the goal
  is to be aware ("La meta es estar consciente"), and stalking is
  defined as an unbending attention on a total result.
- In the perception chapter: the goal of sorcerers is to perceive all
  that is humanly possible.

The English edition adds "the goal of total freedom" as the goal of the
nagual's party, repeated across Parte 3.

**Libertad.** Torres's introduction states that everything a warrior
does must be oriented to the true purpose of the human being, freedom,
and quotes Castaneda that freedom is an individual choice each person
must fight for. Parte 3 frames total freedom as the collective goal of
the lineage.

**The four mitades.** Not found. Searched "mitad", "mitades", "la mitad
del", "la mitad de la" in the Spanish text and "half", "halves" in the
English text; every hit is ordinary usage ("half the work is done",
"half his time"). Google Books returns zero results for "la mitad del"
with Torres as author. The second book could not be full-text searched.

## Second book, El secreto de la serpiente emplumada

Catalog descriptions say it covers Torres's time with a group of
healers and sorcerers, ceremonies including one to call down the
Plumed Serpent, and further Castaneda conversations. Torres notes he
mixes the healers' vocabulary with Castaneda's nagualism. No table of
contents was reachable. Reviews are mixed on authenticity; one reviewer
questions how close Torres was to Castaneda's circle. Recommend the main
flow obtain the Kindle or paper edition if the mitades must be traced.

## Caveats

- Torres was not a formal apprentice; he says he attended some lectures
  and private talks from 1984 onward and reconstructed conversations.
  Treat his Castaneda quotations as Torres's rendering, not Castaneda's
  verbatim words.
- Public PDF copies were used only for keyword search and short
  summaries; no bulk text is reproduced here or in primary.
