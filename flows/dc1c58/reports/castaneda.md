not an endorsement of the factual status of the narrated teachings.

## Identity and scope: what the sources can support

**Observed.** Carlos Castañeda's *The Teachings of Don Juan: A Yaqui Way of
Knowledge* was published by University of California Press in 1968. The current
UC Press description calls the book controversial and explicitly permits the two
literary frames: “**Whether read as ethnographic fact or creative fiction**.”
[UC Press](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/the-teachings-of-don-juan/hardcover)
records the current 2016 edition as ISBN 978-0-520-29076-1.

**Observed.** The UCLA archive finding aid records Castañeda's 1973 anthropology
dissertation and requires permission to publish or quote archival materials.
[Online Archive of California](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt9c6023ds_aspace_ref173_kbm)

**Scholarly assessment.** A 2013 article in *HAU: Journal of Ethnographic Theory*
reports that doubts about the ethnography arose early and that de Mille's 1976
critique identified irregularities suggesting fiction; it says Castañeda's
anthropological reputation did not recover.
[Jensen, “Two forms of the outside”](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.14318/hau3.3.013)
This is a scholarly claim about reception, not a proof that every narrated
encounter is invented. For Book of Sol, treat the corpus as twentieth-century
literature and a claimed teaching-system; do not call it verified Yaqui, Toltec,
or Indigenous doctrine.

**Observed about Torres.** Library and publisher metadata identify Armando Torres
as author of *Encuentros con el nagual: conversaciones con Carlos Castañeda*:
Editorial Alba, 2002, 269 pp., ISBN 9789685671002
[Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Encuentros_con_el_nagual.html?id=WWQkAQAAIAAJ),
with Spanish editions catalogued by Dialnet (Madrid: Nuevo Amanecer, 2004; and
Barcelona: RBA, 2006).
[Dialnet](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/autor?codigo=1617268)
The book presents conversations and recollections; its own asserted encounters
are not independently confirmed by these catalogues. Attribute statements as
Torres's account, never as Castañeda's recorded speech or Mexican tradition.

The catalogue-backed titles most easily confused with the supplied forms are:

- Castañeda, *Relatos de poder* / *Tales of Power* (1974/1975 in common listings),
  then *El segundo anillo de poder* / *The Second Ring of Power* (1977),
  *El don del águila* / *The Eagle's Gift* (1981), *El fuego interno* /
  *The Fire from Within* (1984), and *El conocimiento silencioso* /
  *The Power of Silence* (1987). A UNAM bibliography supplies Spanish publication
  anchors for the latter sequence.
  [UNAM thesis bibliography](https://tesiunamdocumentos.dgb.unam.mx/pd1999/271850/271850.pdf)
- *The Power of Silence* is **not** the published English title for `El poder del
  silencio`; the Spanish edition listed by the UNAM bibliography is *El
  conocimiento silencioso*. Do not silently “correct” either title in an
  attribution.
- Torres, *Encuentros con el nagual* / *Encounters with the Nagual* is the
  documented bilingual pair. English Google Books metadata lists topical chapters
  including *Self-Importance*, *Awareness of Death*, *Recapitulation*, and
  *Practicing Silence*.
  [English edition metadata](https://books.google.com/books/about/Encounters_with_the_nagual.html?id=BSb3DwAAQBAJ)

## Usable thematic map for original prose

This table is a map of the texts' internal vocabulary, not a claim that it is
historically Yaqui or Toltec vocabulary.

| Composition concern | Castañeda-facing terms | Torres-facing terms | Writing guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Power | *power*, personal power, impeccability; **poder**, *impecabilidad* | *energía*, *poder*, *drenaje energético* | Power is not equivalent to domination, productivity, or an evidence-based psychological construct. |
| Willpower | *will* appears in early work; later framing more often uses **intent / intento** | *intento*, practice, discipline | Prefer **intent / intento** where that is the source word; do not substitute “willpower” and claim it translates it. |
| Goals | a path, knowledge, freedom | *objetivo*, task, final choice, freedom | `La meta` can be original: the sources do not establish it as a fixed canonical term. |
| Warrior | warrior, man of knowledge; **guerrero**, *hombre de conocimiento* | *camino del guerrero*, *diálogo de guerreros* | The figure is literary and aspirational; avoid converting it into a universal Indigenous type. |
| Seer / sorcerer | **vidente / brujo**, *ver*, *nagual*, *tonal* | *videntes*, *brujo*, *nagual* | English **sorcerer** is a conventional translation of *brujo* in this corpus, not a neutral ethnographic gloss. |
| Freedom | freedom / **libertad** | freedom / **libertad** | State only what the text makes of freedom; it must not be presented as a clinical or political promise. |

**Observed textual anchor.** Google Books' contents for the 1984 Simon & Schuster
edition of *The Fire from Within* list “**Stalking Intent and the Dreaming
Position**,” which confirms *intent* as a central English rubric rather than an
editorial synonym for willpower.
[Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Fire_from_Within.html?id=CxAVAAAAYAAJ)

**Observed textual anchor.** The 2009 English *Wheel of Time* metadata, a
Castañeda-selected quotation collection, includes this brief attributed sentence:
“**a warrior laughs and laughs**.”
[Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Wheel_Of_Time.html?id=aqz4lHgxNNYC)
It is suitable only as a short epigraph with edition/page verification before
publication; it is not a license to reproduce surrounding passages.

## Composition directions supported by this dossier

1. **La meta / The Aim.** Make the goal a chosen orientation or a question, not a
   supposedly canonical Castañeda doctrine. The counterpoint can be the source
   distinction between *intento* and ordinary willpower.
2. **El guerrero / The Warrior.** Rework the extant Book of Sol article as original
   prose that cites a named edition. Keep “warrior” as a literary posture facing
   fear, mortality, attention, and self-importance; do not present its practices
   as factual cultural anthropology.
3. **Las mitades.** The four supplied `mitad` headings can form an explicitly new
   bilingual sequence: the *brujo* names encounter with the unknown, the
   *guerrero* discipline, the *vidente* perception, and *libertad* the horizon.
   This is an inference from the corpus's recurring motifs, not its native
   taxonomy. State that authorship clearly in an introduction or note.

## Editorial constraints for the later writer

- Cite a specific Spanish or English edition for any quotation, including page
  number. The Spanish title itself is not proof of a particular wording in an
  English edition.
- Make fresh English prose from the thematic map. Do not translate an unverified
  Spanish passage and label it an established Castañeda translation.
- Keep quotations short. The two supplied quotations above are each well under
  25 words; no book text was bulk copied for this dossier.
- Separate source voice from composition voice with phrasing such as “In
  Castañeda's narrative…” or “Torres recounts…”.
- Avoid claims about Yaqui or Mexican Indigenous belief unless supported by a
  relevant Indigenous or scholarly source outside this literary corpus.
