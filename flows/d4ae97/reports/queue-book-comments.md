The first two proposals are brilliant in content but they're misdirected. Here's an important concept which will go in spirit: anything that the living has explicitly reviewed and approved is much more valuable than agent-inferred output or machine-inferred output.

We can't just go from the psyche taking its time: "this, that, that's this. This is the highest cost," right? The living's time has the highest cost so we can't lose track of the fact. We have to know that something has been explicitly approved by the living, which is why the psyche data exists.

The golden rule is: thou shalt not touch the psyche data unless the living has explicitly approved an edit, which he has reviewed. That's the goal, that's our goal, and that's our most valuable thing. If we desecrate it by letting somebody else edit it or if we lose it by editing it in another place, then we're bleeding. We're bleeding all of our wealth.

It's a really important spirit principle that, essentially for now, we need to just make that explicitly important in every flow. Nobody gets the idea of not putting psyche data in where the psyche data is, so that we know that's what it is, or losing it by editing it, by taking the approval of the psyche and putting it somewhere else.

Let's also have audit, reviews, and proposal, right? Whenever there's a review there's a proposal to correct, add, or remove some vision. It has to do with how we approach this particular problem from now on so that, if we instruct properly, it isn't going to require our time and energy from the living to deal with, because the approach actually works. The vision becomes implementation and then the approach works and so on. Eventually the machine has adapted to how the living wants it to behave and so it knows how to deal with more and more different types of situations.
